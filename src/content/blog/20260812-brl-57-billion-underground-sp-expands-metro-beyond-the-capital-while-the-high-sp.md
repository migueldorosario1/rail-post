While the São Paulo metro expands across eight lines and surpasses the capital's boundaries, the SP-RJ high-speed train project remains on paper, revealing two distinct speeds in Brazilian railway policy.

While the high-speed train piles up promises, the São Paulo metro drills through the ground meter by meter. For the first time in history, the metro network crosses the capital's boundaries. The contrast between what advances in São Paulo's underground and what remains on paper reveals two distinct speeds in Brazilian railway policy.

The São Paulo state government announced an investment of BRL 57 billion to expand the metro network by about 50 km, plus BRL 14 billion for 22 km of railway network. This is the largest metro investment in the country's history, with works underway on eight lines, purchase of 63 new trains, and unprecedented metropolitan expansion to municipalities outside the capital.

Line 2-Green, for example, is being extended to Guarulhos, with 13.8 km in length and eight new stations. Phase 1, to Penha, is already 55% complete. According to STM (Metropolitan Transport Secretariat), the stretch to Guarulhos will have BRL 2.59 billion budgeted for 2026.

Line 15-Silver, a monorail connecting the East Zone to Vila Prudente, also received a significant budget. For 2026, BRL 1.03 billion is planned for the section between Ipiranga and Hospital Cidade Tiradentes, aiming to expand the region's transport capacity.

Line 17-Gold, which will connect Congonhas Airport to Morumbi Station, is scheduled to open in March 2026. The section will have BRL 836.3 million in 2026, mainly for completing the works.

One of the most ambitious projects is the extension of Line 4-Yellow to Taboão da Serra, with 3.3 km in tunnels and two new stations. Excavations began in March 2026, with completion expected in 2028 and opening in 2030. The secant shafts of the future Taboão da Serra Station will be about 35 meters deep, lined with shotcrete and wire mesh.

During the works, the concessionaire Motiva Trilhos carries out permanent geotechnical monitoring, using inclinometers, surface benchmarks, and pumps to control the water table. This monitoring ensures the safety of the teams, nearby properties, and the station structure itself.

In addition, the São Paulo Metro is modernizing its systems, installing CBTC (Communication-Based Train Control) and platform screen doors (PSD) on existing lines. These technologies increase train control precision and passenger safety.

Meanwhile, the SP-RJ high-speed train, a 417 km project with a maximum speed of 320 km/h, remains on paper. Although promised since 2007, the project still has not a single meter of track built. TAV Brasil, the company responsible, projects operations to start in 2032, but construction was postponed from 2027 to 2028.

The estimated cost of the high-speed train is BRL 60 billion, with tickets expected to cost between BRL 300 and BRL 500. The project still has no environmental license, as the EIA/Rima (Environmental Impact Study and Report) has not been submitted to Ibama.

Experts question the economic viability of the high-speed train, citing the lack of technical experience and the country's fiscal crisis. Cláudio Frischtak, economist and president of Inter.B Consultoria Internacional de Negócios, states that Brazil has never had construction experience with high-speed trains. "Our railway engineering operates on another level, focused on freight trains," he points out.

Skepticism around the high-speed train stems from the project's history, which was an election campaign banner for the PT (Workers' Party). Over the years, the proposal passed through bodies such as the Planning and Logistics Company (EPL), without leaving the drawing board.

In contrast, the expansion of the São Paulo metro demonstrates a more efficient and tangible railway policy. With massive investments and works underway, the São Paulo metro is advancing, improving urban mobility and citizens' quality of life. The future of rail transport in Brazil seems more clearly defined in São Paulo's underground, where construction advances, than on the SP-RJ axis, where promises continue without materializing.
