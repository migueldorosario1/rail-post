The state of São Paulo invests R$ 57 billion to expand the subway network, transforming urban mobility and boosting development.

The government of the state of São Paulo announced the largest investment in subway expansion in history, with R$ 57 billion allocated for the construction of approximately 50 km of subway and R$ 14 billion for 22 km of railway network (CPTM), totaling 72 additional km of rail transport. When technology organizes space, society gains time to live. The project aims to improve mobility and quality of life in the metropolitan region, connecting various neighborhoods and municipalities.

Simultaneous works will cover Lines 2-Green, 4-Yellow, 6-Orange, 15-Silver, 17-Gold, 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire and 13-Jade, all part of the Metrô SP and CPTM systems. Line 4-Yellow will have its planned extension to reach Guarulhos, a historic milestone for the system. This extension promises to further integrate the capital with neighboring municipalities, facilitating the daily commute of thousands of workers and residents.

Line 6-Orange, one of the main novelties, will have 15.3 km underground and 15 stations, connecting Brasilândia (North Zone) to São Joaquim (Center). Partial operation is scheduled for October 2026, with full operation in 2027, through a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) with the concessionaire Linha Uni (Acciona). This project is essential to decongest traffic in the North Zone and Center, areas that suffer from overcrowding and a lack of efficient public transport options.

Line 17-Gold, another important addition, will have 6.7 km of commercial extension and eight stations, connecting Congonhas Airport to Morumbi Station (Line 9-Emerald). This strategic connection will allow passengers to move quickly between the airport and other important points in the city, significantly reducing travel time and increasing air transport efficiency.

Line 15-Silver, a monorail, will also undergo expansions, serving Ipiranga, Boa Esperança and Jacu Pêssego, with integration to Line 2-Green and Line 10-Turquoise (CPTM). This line has already demonstrated its effectiveness in connecting distant and densely populated regions, and the expansion promises to further expand that capacity, directly benefiting residents of these areas.

Governor Tarcísio de Freitas enacted a law authorizing loans of up to US$ 425 million (approximately R$ 2.2 billion) for the expansion of Lines 2-Green and 6-Orange. The financing was obtained from multilateral financial institutions, private banks and national and international development agencies, ensuring the economic sustainability of the project. As pointed out by <a href='https://timesbrasil.com.br/brasil/sp-anuncia-mais-de-r-50-bi-para-expansao-do-metro/' target='_blank'>portal Times Brasil</a>, this investment represents a firm commitment to urban development and improving the quality of life of São Paulo residents.

Line 2-Green, currently expanding to Penha (East Zone), starting from Vila Madalena, is an example of how intermodal integration can be effectively implemented. The extension of this line will allow more people to have access to fast and reliable transportation, reducing dependence on buses and cars, and consequently, reducing pollution and street congestion.

The São Paulo Metro expansion project is not limited to the capital, but also includes CPTM, with investments in lines such as 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire and 13-Jade. These lines, which are already essential for mobility in the metropolitan region, will receive improvements and expansions, further strengthening the rail transport network and providing a smoother and more comfortable experience for users.

The impact of this R$ 71 billion investment goes beyond the simple expansion of kilometers of tracks. It represents a serious commitment to modernizing urban infrastructure, reducing travel time, improving quality of life and promoting economic development. By investing in cutting-edge technology and long-term projects, the São Paulo government is signaling a vision for the future where rail transport is the backbone of urban mobility, contributing to the construction of more sustainable and livable cities.

With the execution of these projects, São Paulo will take a significant leap in terms of mobility, becoming an example for other Brazilian and Latin American cities. The integration of different modes, energy efficiency and the capacity to transport large volumes of passengers are just some of the benefits that will be perceived in the coming years. Investment in rail transport is not just a matter of infrastructure, but also a strategy for urban and social development, placing people at the center of planning decisions.
