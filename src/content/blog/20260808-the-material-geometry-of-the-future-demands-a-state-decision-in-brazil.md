While the country signs agreements and studies connecting oceans, the real expansion of railways still depends on combining engineering, Chinese money, and political courage to break the road-centric inertia.

<p>Railways are the material geometry of the future. When a nation decides to deploy a high-capacity railway, it is not just solving a logistics problem: it is redesigning the productivity map, reorganizing the territory, and defining who will participate in the global value chains in the following decade.</p>

<p>Brazil recently signed a memorandum of understanding with China to study a railway that will connect the port of Ilhéus in Bahia to the port of Chancay in Peru, crossing the continent from the Atlantic to the Pacific. The project plans to cross Bahia, Goiás, Mato Grosso, Rondônia, and Acre before entering Peruvian territory and reaching the terminal inaugurated in 2024 with Chinese financing under the Belt and Road Initiative.</p>

<p>Peruvian government projections indicate that the new route can reduce the average freight transport time from Brazil to Asia from 40 to 28 days. The <a href="https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/08/ferrovia-para-ligar-o-atlantico-ao-pacifico-veja-o-que-se-sabe-ate-agora-do-projeto-do-brasil-com-a-china.ghtml" target="_blank">technical partnership signed between Infra S.A. and the China Railway Economic Research and Planning Institute</a> has an initial duration of five years and includes technical, economic, and environmental feasibility studies.</p>

<p>This movement occurs in parallel with the launch of the National Railway Plan, which provides for approximately R$ 100 billion in investments to expand the network by 4.7 thousand kilometers. <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/" target="_blank">The package includes five major projects</a>: the East-West Corridor, the extension of the North-South Railway, the Southeast Railway Ring, the Transnordestina, and the Ferrogrão.</p>

<p>The math, however, remains relentless. Each kilometer of railway costs, on average, R$ 27 million — more than three times the cost of a highway, and the return can take decades to materialize. The Brazilian railway geography reflects this difficulty: in 2025, 73% of all cargo transported by rail was iron ore, and only 19% corresponded to agricultural products.</p>

<p>Two companies dominate the scene. Vale alone handled 301 million tons on its railways last year, operating in a vertically integrated model that links mine, railway, and port. Rumo, the logistics arm of Cosan, focuses on transporting grains from the Midwest through the Malha Norte corridor to the port of Santos.</p>

<p>To burst this bubble and bring agribusiness and the processing industry to the rails, some state support seems unavoidable. The federal government is negotiating a special line of financing at BNDES with terms of up to 60 years and extended grace periods during construction, when the railway does not generate revenue.</p>

<p>International capital, particularly Chinese, emerges as a centerpiece in this financial engineering. <a href="https://investnews.com.br/negocios/brasil-tenta-de-novo-expandir-as-ferrovias-mas-ainda-falta-combinar-com-os-chineses/" target="_blank">Five Chinese companies are already studying railway and highway projects in the country</a>, according to Transport Minister Renan Filho. CCCC, a Chinese state-owned infrastructure company, has established itself in Brazil with a stake in the São Luís Port and is following railway auctions with special interest in the Fico-Fiol corridor.</p>

<p>CRRC, the world's largest train manufacturer, is setting up a factory in Araraquara with an initial investment of R$ 50 million to produce rolling stock for the São Paulo Metro and the Intercity Train. The logic is simple: 70% of the soybeans and corn imported by China come from Brazil, and organizing Brazilian logistics is a matter of food security for Beijing.</p>

<p>While the megaprojects await auctions and conciliations in the Supreme Federal Court, an alternative model is beginning to show concrete results. The Chilean company Arauco started construction in February of the first fully private railway branch under the authorization regime, connecting its pulp mill in Inocência, Mato Grosso do Sul, to Rumo's network and from there to the port of Santos.</p>

<p>It is 54 kilometers of railway with an investment of R$ 2.4 billion, designed to remove around 190 daily truck trips from the region's highways and reduce CO2 emissions in transport by up to 94%. <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/infra/apos-cinco-anos-primeira-ferrovia-por-autorizacao-comeca-a-sair-do-papel/" target="_blank">The first authorized railway to get off the drawing board</a> comes five years after the government announced dozens of projects under the model created by Provisional Measure 1,065.</p>

<p>Of the applications submitted, most withdrew or had their deadlines expire before obtaining authorization from ANTT. Currently, 42 projects have permission from the regulatory agency, and Director-General Guilherme Sampaio assesses that the model is beginning to work as another option on the Brazilian railway menu.</p>

<p>What is at stake is not just logistical efficiency, but Brazil's ability to position itself as a protagonist in the reorganization of global supply chains. Each ton transported by rail instead of road represents an engineering decision that reverberates in export competitiveness and the decarbonization of the economy.</p>

<p>The material geometry of the future demands courage to break the road-centric inertia that has dominated Brazilian infrastructure thinking for seven decades. Without State decision and strategic partnerships, railways will remain confined to iron ore while the opportunities of the new world economic geography pass by.</p>
