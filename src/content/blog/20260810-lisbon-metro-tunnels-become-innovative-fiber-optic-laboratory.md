Iscte project uses the yellow line to test fibers capable of multiplying data transmission capacity sevenfold, while the new Circular Line advances toward 2027.

Every great city reveals its intelligence by the way it moves people. Lisbon has just added a surprising layer to this equation by transforming the tunnels of the metro's yellow line into an innovative fiber optic testing center.

The project is led by Iscte – Lisbon University Institute and represents a total investment of 1.4 million euros. Of that amount, around 588 thousand euros come from the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF) through the Regional Programme Lisbon 2030 – Technological Infrastructures.

The initiative creates an authentic underground ring of innovation, running through tunnels and stations of the yellow line with next-generation fiber optic cables. The core objective is to test fibers capable of multiplying data transmission capacity up to seven times compared to the current generation, which is already dangerously approaching saturation.

The vice-rector of Iscte, Jorge Rodrigues da Costa, explained that the idea arose from a challenge posed by a German manufacturer. According to <a href="https://www.ccdr-lvt.pt/2025/11/iscte-transforma-o-metro-de-lisboa-num-laboratorio-vivo-de-inovacao-urbana/" target="_blank">information from CCDR LVT</a>, the coincidence that the Iscte campus is situated exactly above a metro line made it the perfect location to test this technology under real and unique conditions on the planet.

The rector of Iscte, Maria de Lurdes Rodrigues, highlighted that the living laboratory created has no parallel in Portugal or Europe. She emphasized the wide-ranging economic impact that projects of this nature generate, attracting talent, companies, and international investment to the Portuguese capital.

Almost all the world's data traffic travels through fiber optics, and the current generation is becoming saturated. The underground laboratory in Lisbon is thus positioned as a test bed with the potential to scale enormously and transform the city into a global geographical and technological reference in optical communications.

In parallel with this invisible revolution in the tunnels, the <a href="https://projetos.metrolisboa.pt/expansao/" target="_blank">Lisbon Metro Expansion Plan</a> advances with the creation of the Circular Line, connecting the Rato station to Cais do Sodré. The new section totals 1,900 meters in length, two new stations — Estrela and Santos — and a total investment of 344.2 million euros, including the modernization of 26 train units and the implementation of the CBTC (Communication-Based Train Control) system.

The completion forecast for the Circular Line is the first quarter of 2027. When operational, the new rail ring will densify the metro offering in the city center and directly connect the Cascais rail services and the Tejo river services to the central axis and the Sintra, Azambuja, and Setúbal lines, without the need for transfers.

The Circular Line project forecasts an increase of 3.1 million passengers per year on the network. It is estimated that 3,380 people will stop using their cars daily, representing a reduction of 4,150 tons of CO2 in the first year of operation alone, a concrete contribution to the fight against climate change.

The technical solution adopted includes the construction of a new connecting viaduct of 158 meters at Campo Grande to close the ring between the current Green and Yellow lines. A second viaduct, 428 meters long, will connect the Campo Grande–Odivelas section to the Telheiras area, preserving the full operability of the existing viaducts.

While Lisbon tests the future of optical communications in its tunnels, Brazil is also advancing large-scale projects. In São Paulo, the expansion of the metro network is ongoing, with a focus on improving and expanding urban infrastructure. The city demonstrates that 21st-century metro infrastructure is not limited to transporting people: it acts as a platform for technological innovation and an inducer of industrial development.

Lisbon's yellow line laboratory and São Paulo's metro expansion projects represent two sides of the same phenomenon. The urban underground, once seen merely as a passageway, is consolidating itself as a strategic territory for economic competitiveness, environmental sustainability, and the technological sovereignty of cities.
