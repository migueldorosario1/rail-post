Brazil breaks decades of stagnation and puts rails at the center of its logistics strategy, with broad-gauge expansion, serial auctions and a rail bridge between the Atlantic and the Pacific.

Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy and direction.

The federal government is preparing to launch the National Railway Plan, a mega-investment of approximately R$100 billion that promises to reconfigure the country's logistics geography. The initiative foresees expanding the network by 4,700 kilometers through five major projects, linking the East-West, North-South and Transnordestina corridors into a single system designed for the export of grains and ores, especially to Asia.

The plan was presented to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by the Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, and only awaits final adjustments to be made official. According to <a href='https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/' target='_blank'>CNN Brasil</a>, the federal government intends to cover 20% to 30% of the contributions in each project, using resources obtained from the renegotiation of railway concessions to guarantee the economic viability of the works.

The East-West Corridor is the backbone of the proposal, uniting the West-East Integration Railway (Fiol) and the Central-West Integration Railway (Fico) over 2,400 kilometers of tracks. Fiol departs from Ilhéus, Bahia, while Fico advances through Goiás to Lucas do Rio Verde, in the agricultural heart of Mato Grosso, directly connecting the Central-West's production to the Atlantic coast.

Another strategic axis is the extension of the North-South Railway by 477 kilometers to the port of Vila do Conde, in Pará. Currently, the North-South line ends in Açailândia, Maranhão, where it connects to the Carajás Railway, but the saturation of this line — used primarily for ore — requires an alternative dedicated to grain outflow through the Northern Arc.

The Transnordestina, which has accumulated over a decade of delays, also gains a new horizon with delivery projected between 2026 and 2027. The federal plan also includes the concession of the railway connection to the North-South line in Estreito, adding another 600 kilometers of tracks to the Northeast logistics system and creating a continuous corridor to the region's ports.

In the Southeast, the Southeast Railway Ring plans 300 kilometers linking Vitória, in Espírito Santo, to Itaboraí, in Rio de Janeiro. This work will allow integration between the Vitória-Minas Railway (EFVM) network and the network operated by MRS Logística, unblocking one of the main intermodality bottlenecks in the country's most industrialized region.

The most ambitious — and also the most sensitive — project is Ferrogrão, with 933 kilometers between Sinop (MT) and Itaituba (PA). The route faces legal challenges in the Federal Supreme Court due to potential impact on conservation units, and the project is under analysis by IBAMA. Despite the challenges, the government seeks to move forward with the bidding.

All the mentioned corridors share a decisive technical characteristic: the 1.60-meter broad gauge, a standard that allows greater stability and load capacity per train. This engineering choice is no mere detail, as it defines the interoperability of the network and the energy efficiency of long-distance transport, reducing the cost per ton-kilometer.

While structuring the National Railway Plan, the Brazilian government is establishing an international partnership that could reshape the continent's trade flows. Although no specific memorandum has been signed in 2025, discussions on railway cooperation between Brazil and China are advancing. The port of Chancay, inaugurated in 2024 by Chinese President Xi Jinping, is part of the Belt and Road Initiative, the New Silk Road. Projections indicate that the new route could reduce the travel time of Brazilian cargo to Asia from 40 to 28 days, a saving that directly interests agribusiness and mining.

Although Brazil has not formally joined the New Silk Road, bilateral cooperation in the railway sector has entered a concrete planning phase. The National Secretary of Railway Transport, Leonardo Ribeiro, described the discussions as the first step in a technical and diplomatic journey to bring continents closer and reduce distances.

The execution schedule provides for eight railway auctions in 2026, combining instruments such as concession, rebidding, early renewal and public call. The Minas Rio Corridor, which would be the first tender of the year, had its notice postponed, but the Ministry of Transport expects to publish it by May so that the auction takes place in August.

For smaller networks, the so-called shortlines, ANTT bets on public call as the main tool. According to the agency's director-general, Guilherme Sampaio, a study conducted with the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) mapped corridors currently abandoned or returned, with the intention of reactivating them using a model similar to the port sector, with a notice, reference project and analysis by the Federal Court of Audits.

The West Network and the South Network, whose contracts expire respectively in July 2025 and February 2027, also join the rebidding queue. The South Network has been divided into three lots — Paraná-SC Corridor, Rio Grande and Mercosur — to increase investor appeal, a decision that drew criticism from the Federal Public Ministry, but which the government defends as necessary to unlock the country's largest railway.

The strategy of combining early renewals with new auctions allowed the Lula government to already close billion-dollar deals with Rumo, MRS Logística and Vale. The mining company, in particular, committed to paying up to R$17 billion for the extension of the Carajás Railway and Vitória-Minas Railway contracts, funds that feed back into the expansion plan itself.

Intermodality, a word so often repeated in official speeches, begins to take on real engineering contours in this new railway cycle. Connecting gauges, integrating ports, linking waterways and reducing dependence on road transport for heavy loads are goals that move from the planning stage to the bidding and construction phase.

If the Ministry of Transport's schedule is confirmed, 2026 could mark the largest movement in the Brazilian railway sector since the Fiol 1 auction held in 2021. The combination of public investment, private capital and international partnership with the world's leading power in railway technology — China — suggests that the country is finally treating rails as a vector of logistical sovereignty and not as a museological heritage from the 19th century.

Modernity, after all, runs on rails. And Brazil seems determined to make up for lost time.
