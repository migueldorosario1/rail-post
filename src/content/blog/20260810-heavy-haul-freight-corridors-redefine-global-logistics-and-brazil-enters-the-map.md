From India to Paraná, high-capacity rails, green bonds and billions in investment reposition bulk transport as a vector of decarbonization and competitiveness.

Each 100-wagon train gliding on broad-gauge rails replaces 357 trucks on the roads—and with them, part of the emissions that Brazil has promised the world to reduce. The figure, drawn from Nova Ferroeste's demand studies, encapsulates the silent logic driving the global convergence of heavy-haul freight rail corridors: energy efficiency, industrial scale and structural reduction of logistics costs.

It is not about railway romanticism, but heavy engineering and financial mathematics applied to export chains. While the global freight rail infrastructure market is projected to jump from US$ 43.6 billion in 2026 to US$ 64.5 billion in 2036, with a CAGR of 4.0%, Brazil appears with a projection of 4.8% per year, anchored on projects such as Nova Ferroeste and the Central-West Integration Railway, FICO, as pointed out by the analysis of consultancy Fact.MR in its global report.

The World Bank acts as a financial and technical catalyst for this movement, currently supporting 15 railway projects totaling US$ 4.5 billion, with expansion forecast to exceed US$ 6 billion over the next five years. The institution does not just finance rails: it structures guarantees, concession models and modal shift mechanisms that turn freight corridors into development policy.

In India, the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor (EDFC) built 1,200 km of dedicated tracks between 2011 and 2024, quadrupling daily freight train capacity—from 32 to 130 pairs per day. Each 1 km-long train replaces 72 trucks, generating savings of US$ 58 million in logistics costs and projecting a cumulative reduction of 13.19 million tonnes of CO₂ equivalent by 2042, representing 57% of 2010 emission levels, according to data compiled by the <a href="https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2025/08/28/transforming-logistics-the-power-of-rail-investment" target="_blank">World Bank in its results brief</a>.

DFCCIL, the Indian state-owned company that operates the corridor, raised US$ 100 million from Japanese bank MUFG with a guarantee from MIGA, the World Bank's Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency, in the entity's first commercial operation. The country now studies the Hybrid Annuity Model for the East-West Freight corridor, connecting Surat to Dankuni with private participation and shared risk.

Brazil enters this arena with projects that combine heavy-haul scale and green financial engineering. Nova Ferroeste, with 1,304 km of broad gauge, a R$ 29.4 billion investment and a 99-year concession, projects to move 38 million tonnes in the first year of full operation, with 26 million tonnes on the main segment alone. The auction on the B3 exchange, scheduled for the second half of 2025, will transfer to the winning consortium the responsibility to build and operate the corridor linking Maracaju, in Mato Grosso do Sul, to the Port of Paranaguá.

The project is eligible for Green Bonds issuance, instruments that link the cost of capital to verifiable environmental targets, as detailed by the coordinator of Paraná's state railway plan, Luiz Henrique Fagundes, in an interview with the Modal Connection portal. The financial modeling combines initial public investment with long-term private operation, an arrangement that allows the consortium to access cheaper funding provided it delivers measurable emission reductions.

The hopper wagons that will travel the route will carry soybeans, corn and refrigerated containers, replacing trucks that currently congest the BR-277 highway on the descent of Serra do Mar. The short line concept, widespread in the United States and adapted to the Brazilian context, organizes smaller branch lines that connect production hubs to the main trunks, multiplying the network's capillarity without requiring disproportionate investments in new trunk corridors.

FICO, in turn, advances in the consolidation of the East-West Railway Corridor with 366 km in total, of which 290 km of infrastructure are to be completed by the end of 2025. The delivery of the superstructure of Lot 1 is scheduled for October this year, representing the first major physical milestone of the project, as reported by the <a href="https://www.gov.br/antt/pt-br/assuntos/ultimas-noticias/projeto-da-fico-avanca-e-consolida-corredor-estrategico-para-o-transporte-de-cargas-no-pais" target="_blank">National Land Transport Agency in an official statement</a>.

The railway connects Mara Rosa, in Goiás, to Água Boa, in Mato Grosso, integrating with FIOL and the North-South Railway, with an outlet through the Porto Sul in Bahia. Demand studies prepared by Infra S.A. project average annual growth of 1.9% until 2060, when the corridor could reach 41 million tonnes of freight transported, with soybeans accounting for 46% of projected demand, followed by corn at 28.5%.

Approximately 80% of the forecasted movement will consist of solid vegetable bulk, which requires railway yards sized to receive trains of up to 13,000 tonnes and digital signaling capable of ensuring safe headways between loaded trains. Institutional cooperation between ANTT, Infra S.A., Vale and TCU strengthens the legal certainty of the project, a decisive aspect for attracting private capital in a sector that requires decades of maturation.

The World Bank, which financed the three projects of the Indian EDFC, applies the same logic in Cameroon, where the modernization of 17 switches and 55 railway bridges reduced the container transport cost on the Douala–N'Djamena corridor by more than 7%. In Serbia, the fatality rate per train-kilometer fell from 3.56 in 2019 to 1.89 in 2025, while in Uzbekistan the freight cost for bulk cargo fell by up to 80% with the new Pap–Angren railway.

What unites India, Brazil, Uzbekistan and Serbia is not geographic coincidence, but the understanding that heavy-haul railways are vectors of logistical sovereignty. Every tonne transported on rails rather than asphalt reduces dependence on imported diesel, decreases the fiscal cost of road maintenance and repositions the country in global commodity chains with more competitive margins.

Brazil, which historically abandoned branch lines in favor of asphalt, begins to return to rails the role of protagonists of development. Nova Ferroeste and FICO are not just engineering works: they are laboratories for how to finance, build and operate transport infrastructure with scale, efficiency and decarbonization targets contractually linked to the cost of capital.
