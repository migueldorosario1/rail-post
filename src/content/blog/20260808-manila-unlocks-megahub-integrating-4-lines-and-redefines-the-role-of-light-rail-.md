While the world connects subways, trains, and trams in stations designed for the citizen, Brazil still hesitates to treat rails as the backbone of development.

<p>Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction.</p>
<p>The final approval by the Philippine government for the construction of the Unified Grand Central Station in Quezon City materializes this maxim with surgical precision. The project, highlighted by <a href="https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/philippines/4-rail-lines-1-transfer-manila-finally-clears-mega-transit-hub-that-could-transform-commuting-in-the-philippine-capital-1.500593569" target="_blank">Gulf News</a>, creates a single transfer point between four railway systems that structure the capital Manila.</p>
<p>In the complex, LRT Line 1, the MRT-3 metro, the future MRT-7 line, and the new subway will finally converse under the same roof. Philippine Finance Secretary Frederick Go summed up the investment logic with transparency: 'Railway projects give people their time back.'</p>
<p>The decision of the Investment Coordination Committee removed the last bureaucratic hurdle of a plan that had been dragging on for over a decade. Disputes over location, commercial rights, and expropriations kept the Southeast Asian capital hostage to disconnected terminals and chaotic surface-level transfers.</p>
<p>Now, the hub will allow a citizen to travel from San Jose Del Monte, in Bulacan province, to Cavite, entirely on rails. The expected immediate effect is the migration of millions of passengers from individual road transport to the medium- and high-capacity network, relieving the suffocating traffic of EDSA avenue.</p>
<p>The engineering of the unified station follows the logic of major Asian hubs, such as those in Tokyo, Singapore, or Hong Kong. There, the LRT is not a nostalgic ornament, but the living capillary that feeds the heavy metro trunks, creating a transport ecosystem whose energy and operational efficiency reduces dependence on fossil fuels.</p>
<p>Beyond mass transit, rails are asserting themselves as a logistical tool in France. The <a href="https://www.francemobilites.fr/solutions/tramfret" target="_blank">TramFret</a> platform, developed in cooperation with French competitiveness clusters, recovers end-of-life trams to transport goods in the heart of cities.</p>
<p>The vehicles, logistically retrofitted, circulate between passenger trains during off-peak hours on the existing infrastructure. The decarbonized solution solves two problems with one rail: nighttime idleness of the network and the collapse of last-mile urban logistics, eliminating diesel trucks from historic centers.</p>
<p>TramFret's conceptual leap lies in the mutualization of public assets: the same corridor that carries the worker in the morning supplies commerce at night. It is the maximum application of the industrial principle that rail infrastructure, once installed, allows multiple layers of productivity if there is regulatory and technological intelligence.</p>
<p>In Brazil, while superficial ideological debates delay projects, Bahia is digging its own future in the Subúrbio Ferroviário of Salvador. The LRT that replaced the old suburban train, as documented by <a href="https://bahiaeconomica.com.br/wp/2026/07/22/nos-trilhos-do-futuro-como-o-vlt-redesenha-a-mobilidade-de-areas-historicamente-negligenciadas-de-salvador/" target="_blank">Bahia Econômica</a>, is the largest urban intervention in the territory since the 19th century.</p>
<p>R$ 5.4 billion are invested not only in 43.7 kilometers of track and 50 stops, but in macro-drainage, mangrove recovery, and linear parks. CTB president Eracy Lafuente emphasizes the reinvention of the space: the work plants mangroves and builds a skatepark with Olympic certification where there was previously abandonment.</p>
<p>The project triples the extension of the old service and projects demand of up to 150 thousand daily users, connecting the peripheral fabric to the metro in Águas Claras. The assisted operation between Calçada and Lobato already has seven-car trains running for free, testing power vector control and signaling systems before fare collection.</p>
<p>The planned modal integration in Salvador breaks with the logic of urban segregation that marked the old train. Instead of isolated and precarious transport, the LRT acts as a backbone that articulates bus terminals and the ferry-boat, reducing the daily ordeal of those who depended on two or three transfers to cross the city.</p>
<p>In the heart of the Central Plateau, Brasília seeks an aesthetic and functional leap with the underground LRT on W3 Avenue. The 16-kilometer, 24-station project, reported by <a href="https://olhardanoticia.com.br/vlt-subterraneo-brasilia-tecnologia-aps-mobilidade/" target="_blank">Olhar da Notícia</a>, abandons the overhead catenary in favor of Ground-Level Power Supply (APS) technology.</p>
<p>Conductive segments embedded in the track, already tested in Bordeaux and Dubai, energize the train only as it passes, preserving the monumental scale of the listed urbanism of Lucio Costa and Oscar Niemeyer. The decision proves that it is possible to modernize the mobility of a heritage capital without erecting forests of poles, maintaining pedestrian safety and the visual flow of the Esplanade.</p>
<p>The 30-year private concession model, defended by Governor Ibaneis Rocha, removes the immediate burden from the public treasury for the deployment of 45-meter trainsets capable of carrying up to 560 passengers. By connecting Juscelino Kubitschek Airport to the Asa Sul terminal, the LRT imposes an electric alternative to the radical car-centric model that suffocates the Monumental Axis.</p>
<p>The lesson emanating from Manila, Salvador, French cities, and Brasília is convergent: the Light Rail Vehicle is not a tourist toy or a relic from the past. It operates as the nervous system that orchestrates local capillary and the feeding of large metropolitan trunks, structuring land use and productive life.</p>
<p>While nations that take the climate emergency seriously use LRT to electrify, massify, and organize the territory, Brazil is still crawling in understanding that rails are development technology. We lost decades romanticizing the old tram instead of demanding modern gauge, level boarding, fare integration, and commercial speed that generate real productivity.</p>
<p>Treating rail transport as a State priority means understanding that each kilometer of LRT implemented reorganizes the urban fabric and reduces the hemorrhage of hours stuck in traffic. International experiences show that the intermodal station is not a mere concrete construction, but a multiplier of citizenship, jobs, and real estate appreciation beyond the walls of condominiums.</p>
<p>By demanding from the public authorities projects that include ground-level power supply, light cargo logistics, and environmental requalification, Brazilian society can finally plant the rails of the future on the hard ground of the present. There is no sustainable innovation in cities that insist on running exclusively on tires.</p>
