Lisbon Metro's underground infrastructure has become the most extensive and advanced living laboratory on the planet for next-generation optical communications, strengthening Portugal's strategic position in the European digital economy.

<p>Every great city reveals its intelligence by how it moves people. Lisbon has just elevated this premise to an unexpected level: its metro tunnels now transport, in addition to passengers, the future of global telecommunications.</p>
<p>Lisbon Metro and Iscte — Lisbon University Institute inaugurated the Lisbon Underground Multicore Fiber Ring, named LUMIRing, the world's largest multi-core optical fiber testbed. The ring runs along 26 kilometers of the Blue Line, between Odivelas and Rato stations, leveraging the existing underground infrastructure as a full-scale urban experimentation platform.</p>
<p>The project installed over 1,900 kilometers of multi-core optical fiber in the metro tunnels, of which 728 kilometers are of the same fiber type, ensuring technical uniformity for testing. These fibers, equipped with four and seven cores instead of the conventional single core, exponentially multiply data transmission capacity compared to traditional optical cables.</p>
<p>The LUMIRing architecture transforms Lisbon Metro's network into an operational-scale living laboratory, something no conventional research center could replicate. Train vibration, temperature variations, humidity and electromagnetic interference from signaling systems create stress conditions that no simulated environment can faithfully reproduce.</p>
<p>The scientific coordination of the project is led by Adolfo Cartaxo, a researcher at Iscte, while execution involves major industrial partners such as Germany's Heraeus Covantics and Italy's Tratos Cavi. The international dimension of the consortium reflects the ambition to position Lisbon as a global technology validation hub for next-generation optical communications.</p>
<p>The project has already attracted the interest of global telecom operators, including US-based AT&T, Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone, who are following the results to guide their investments in network capacity expansion. Japan's public agency National Institute of Information and Communication Technology (NICT), a global reference in information technology research, has entered into a collaboration to test multi-core fiber systems on a real scale throughout 2026, as <a href="https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/2025/11/03/metropolitano-de-lisboa-e-iscte-inauguram-maior-banco-de-testes-de-fibra-otica-multinucleo-do-mundo-3/" target="_blank">highlighted by Lisbon Metro</a> in its official statement.</p>
<p>The most immediate practical application for the metro operator lies in the fields of digitalization, traffic control and operational safety. The ability to transmit massive volumes of data in real time can revolutionize predictive monitoring systems, communication between trains and control centers, and the integration of smart sensors throughout the network.</p>
<p>From a transport engineering perspective, the project subverts the traditional logic that views railway tunnels as mere passive conduits for trains. The underground gallery now simultaneously functions as an urban mobility channel and a backbone for technological innovation, extracting double value from every kilometer excavated.</p>
<p>The acting president of Lisbon Metro, Maria Helena Campos, described the initiative as a unique opportunity to test cutting-edge technologies on a real operational scale. She stressed that this is a decisive step in validating multi-core fibers using avant-garde solutions that have been little tested on a global scale.</p>
<p>Iscte's Vice-Rector for Research and Technological Modernization, Jorge Costa, emphasized that the terrestrial testbed will turn Portugal into a global telecommunications hub. He added that the country is already attracting researchers from all over and high-quality scientific production, generating highly skilled jobs based in Lisbon.</p>
<p>The choice of the Blue Line tunnels as a physical support for LUMIRing was not accidental: it is one of the longest and most stable axes of the Lisbon network. The linearity of the route and the depth of the stations offer ideal conditions for continuous cable installation without the interruptions and interference that a surface network would inevitably suffer.</p>
<p>The multi-core fiber concept breaks the paradigm that prevailed for decades in the optics industry, where each cable housed a single physical path for light. By integrating four or seven independent cores within the same fiber, researchers can multiply the transmission rate without increasing the cable diameter or requiring new duct infrastructure.</p>
<p>The traffic density that LUMIRing can carry is equivalent to stacking multiple data highways within the same physical space that previously supported only one lane. This capacity compression has direct implications for supporting services such as ultra-high-definition streaming, telesurgery, autonomous vehicles and urban digital twins that require near-zero latency and virtually unlimited bandwidth.</p>
<p>For a metro network, the gain in internal communication capacity paves the way for fully autonomous trains operating at Grade of Automation 4, where no driver is needed on board. Data transmission between the train and the track could achieve speeds and reliability that current digital radio systems, such as conventional CBTC, simply cannot match.</p>
<p>The financial investment of the project has not yet been publicly detailed, but the scale of the installation — thousands of kilometers of specialty fiber in an underground environment — indicates a significant budget. The expected return, however, transcends the immediate balance sheet and projects onto Portugal's strategic positioning in the European digital economy.</p>
<p>The initiative aligns with the concept of smart and resilient cities, which require layers of connectivity superimposed on the traditional urban fabric. Lisbon Metro demonstrates in practice that digital transformation does not need to wait for new construction or major urban interventions: it can be woven into the interstices of already existing infrastructure.</p>
<p>The Portuguese experience offers a conceptual model that could be replicated in metro networks of other cities, including in Brazil, where systems such as those in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and Salvador have kilometers of tunnels with analogous conditions. The advantage of using metro tunnels as a testbed is that they already have stabilized electrical power, permanent technical access and continuous surveillance, drastically reducing the setup costs of a cutting-edge laboratory.</p>
<p>The knowledge generated by LUMIRing in the coming years will directly influence the technical standards that the International Telecommunication Union will adopt for the next generation of optical networks. Lisbon, which was once the capital of a maritime empire, is now establishing itself as a point of departure for a new era of invisible connections, where light travels along multiple simultaneous paths beneath passengers' feet.</p>
<p>By making its tunnels available for cutting-edge science, Lisbon Metro demonstrates that the vocation of a public transport company can go far beyond moving people from one point to another. Mobility infrastructure ultimately reveals itself as the nervous system upon which the smart city weaves its innovation circuits.</p>
