Record budget of R$5.4 billion in 2026 tries to recover unfulfilled promises since the 2014 World Cup, but private concessions and public debt raise red flags.

<p>Modernity is not abstract. It has substance, gauge, energy, and direction.</p>
<p>In São Paulo's underground, this maxim materializes in tunnels, tracks, and control systems that are beginning to receive the largest volume of resources ever allocated to the city's metro. R$5.4 billion is budgeted for 2026, a record figure which, according to a report by <a href="https://exame.com/brasil/expansao-das-linhas-2-15-e-entrega-da-17-plano-de-r-54-bi-do-metro-de-sp/" target="_blank">Exame</a>, represents a 12% increase over the R$4.8 billion of the previous year.</p>
<p>The figure is impressive, but the euphoria warrants caution. A large part of these works was on paper since before the 2014 World Cup, when the branch of Line 17-Gold was already heralded as a legacy of the mega-event.</p>
<p>Now, with inauguration scheduled for March 2026, the 6.7 km stretch with eight stations between Congonhas Airport and Morumbi Station on Line 9-Emerald emerges as a symbol of a delay that finally tries to repay itself. The São Paulo state government, under Governor Tarcísio de Freitas (Republicans), has allocated R$836.3 million just for this front, according to budget data.</p>
<p>Line 2-Green, which cuts across Avenida Paulista and currently runs from Vila Madalena to Vila Prudente, will absorb the largest slice: R$2.59 billion. The plan is to extend it to Guarulhos, in Greater São Paulo, adding 13.8 km of new tracks and eight stations in a first phase, with five additional ones in the next stage.</p>
<p>It is an equally old promise, which now advances with over 55% of the works completed on the stretch to Penha. However, the metro integration with the second-largest municipality in the state remains dependent on an investment cycle that will extend over several years.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, Line 15-Silver, the monorail of the East Zone, will receive R$1.03 billion in 2026. The current route connects Vila Prudente to Jardim Colonial, but the project aims to reach Ipiranga, Boa Esperança, and Jacu Pêssego, providing connection with Line 10-Turquoise of the Companhia Paulista de Trens Metropolitanos (CPTM).</p>
<p>The budget growth for this mode — higher than the R$629.5 million of 2025 — reflects the urgency of expanding transport capacity in a densely populated region. The purchase of 19 new trains for the line, with 15 units already delivered, is part of the modernization effort.</p>
<p>In addition to the expansion front, the São Paulo metro allocates resources to technological upgrades. The installation of the CBTC (Communication-Based Train Control) system will allow greater precision in the spacing between trains, reducing intervals and increasing the number of available seats.</p>
<p>Platform screen doors, another component of the package, increase safety and operational efficiency. The modernization also includes the order of 44 new trains for lines 1-Blue, 2-Green, and 3-Red, the oldest and most overloaded in the network.</p>
<p>In total, the state government announced an investment of R$57 billion to expand the metro network by approximately 50 km, plus R$14 billion for 22 km of railway network, resulting in an additional 72 km of rail transport. As reported by <a href="https://timesbrasil.com.br/brasil/sp-anuncia-mais-de-r-50-bi-para-expansao-do-metro/" target="_blank">Times Brasil</a>, the schedule also involves works on lines 4-Yellow, 6-Orange, 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire, and 13-Jade.</p>
<p>Line 4-Yellow, in particular, will have an unprecedented extension outside the capital, something never before achieved in the history of the São Paulo metro system. Meanwhile, Line 6-Orange, under construction under a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) with the concessionaire Linha Uni (Acciona), will be 15.3 km with 15 stations, with partial operation scheduled for October 2026 and full operation in 2027.</p>
<p>The logic of concessions, by the way, is the backdrop of the current mobility policy. Governor Tarcísio plans to transfer all state rail lines to the private sector, a move that enables robust investments but raises questions about fares, cross-subsidies, and public control over operations.</p>
<p>Line 17-Gold, for example, was built by the state-owned metro, but will be operated by Motiva Trilhos. The separation between state construction and private operation is beginning to become the rule in the system.</p>
<p>The financing of these ambitions combines state treasury resources with credit operations. In December 2025, Tarcísio enacted a law authorizing loans of up to US$425 million (about R$2.2 billion) from multilateral agencies and private banks, specifically for lines 2-Green and 6-Orange.</p>
<p>The strategy allows accelerating works, but increases public debt at a time of fiscal pressure. Balancing urban urgency with financial responsibility remains the great challenge for Brazil's largest metro network.</p>
<p>While São Paulo moves forward, albeit belatedly, the country watches. The record budget of R$5.4 billion for 2026 signals that rail transport is back on the priority agenda, but the model based on private concessions raises the debate over who truly owns urban mobility.</p>
<p>The federal government, in turn, is preparing to launch the National Railway Plan with an investment of R$100 billion for five major freight projects, as anticipated by <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>, indicating that railways are regaining ground in national planning. Even so, the case of São Paulo is emblematic: a record amount of resources coexists with the shadow of promises that have aged poorly.</p>
<p>The metropolis that houses the country's largest urban concentration is finally experiencing an investment cycle that could redefine the mobility of millions of people. The risk is that the rush to privatize the future puts the public interest in the background.</p>
<p>Modernity, after all, is not just a matter of engineering. It is also a political choice about who finances, who operates, and who controls the right to come and go in Brazilian metropolises.</p>
