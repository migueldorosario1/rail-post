<p>Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy and direction.</p>
<p>In the heart of the Cerrado, between Mara Rosa (GO) and Água Boa (MT), 366 kilometers of tracks materialize this thesis with steel, crushed stone and schedule. The Ferrovia de Integração do Centro-Oeste, FICO, leaves the drawing board to become the first major physical landmark of a corridor designed to redesign Brazilian export logistics.</p>
<p>According to data from the <a href="https://www.gov.br/antt/pt-br/assuntos/ultimas-noticias/projeto-da-fico-avanca-e-consolida-corredor-estrategico-para-o-transporte-de-cargas-no-pais" target="_blank">National Land Transport Agency</a>, about 290 kilometers of infrastructure are expected to be completed by the end of 2025. The delivery of the superstructure of Lot 1, scheduled for October, represents the first major pulse of a railway that is born from an atypical and potent institutional arrangement.</p>
<p>Vale, Infra S.A. and ANTT operate in a governance consortium, with legal certainty anchored in the early extension of the concession contract. The general director of ANTT, Guilherme Theo Sampaio, defined the project as the fruit of dialogue and consensus-seeking, an alignment that transforms planning into execution.</p>
<p>The FICO alignment integrates the East-West Railway Corridor, connecting to the North-South Railway and the future West-East Integration Railway. The route was designed to capture the flow of solid vegetable bulk that today travels mostly on rubber tires, destined for the ports of the North and Northeast.</p>
<p>Demand studies by Infra S.A. reveal the anatomy of future cargo: approximately 46% soybeans, 28.5% corn and 15.3% fertilizers, with soybean meal completing 6.2% of the volume. The projection indicates an average annual growth of 1.9%, reaching 41 million useful tons per year in 2060.</p>
<p>Each component of this freight corridor operates as a cog in a system that reduces the Brazil cost and expands the competitiveness of agribusiness. Transshipment terminals distributed along the route will allow trucks to travel short distances, feeding high-capacity heavy-haul trains.</p>
<p>FICO is not an isolated case. It inaugurates a new Brazilian railway phase, in which greenfield projects cease to be mirages of lost decades and start to obey contracts, licenses and verifiable physical schedules.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.mundotrilhos.com/blog/20260712-nova-ferroeste-projeta-corredor-de-38-milhoes-de-toneladas-e-reconfigura-a-logis/" target="_blank">Nova Ferroeste</a> illustrates this same movement with 1,304 kilometers of tracks between Maracaju (MS) and Paranaguá (PR), an investment of R$ 29.4 billion and a 99-year concession. The project projects 38 million tons already in the first year of full operation, combining heavy engineering, a 28% reduction in export costs and eligibility for green bonds.</p>
<p>The coordinator of the Paraná state railway plan, Luiz Henrique Fagundes, highlighted that each 100-wagon train will replace 357 trucks on the roads. The financial modeling with Green Bonds issuance signals harmony between the capital market and heavy infrastructure, something rare in Brazilian history.</p>
<p>While Paraná builds its broad-gauge backbone, Minas Gerais positions itself as the passage territory for another megaproject: the JK Railway, by Petrocity. There will be 2,100 kilometers of new tracks, with EF-033 crossing 41 municipalities in Minas Gerais to the Port of São Mateus, in Espírito Santo.</p>
<p>The railway investment by <a href="https://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/economia/2022/03/08/internas_economia,1350823/megaprojeto-cria-corredor-de-exportacao-que-passa-por-minas-gerais.shtml" target="_blank">Petrocity</a> is estimated at R$ 23 billion, with transshipment terminals planned for Unaí, Montes Claros, Grão Mogol and Teófilo Otoni. The group's president, José Roberto Barbosa da Silva, states that the costs are being borne by investors, without depending on BNDES.</p>
<p>The JK Railway will operate with mixed gauge, connecting the Center-West to the coast of Espírito Santo, with the EF-456 branch departing from Ipatinga and capturing the mineral and steel production of the Vale do Aço. The forecast is to complete the environmental licensing projects in five years to then begin heavy construction.</p>
<p>Looking at the whole, one can see that FICO, Nova Ferroeste and JK Railway form a triangle of investments that exceeds R$ 75 billion in new tracks. Each project responds to a distinct production geography, but all share the same logic: return to the train the preponderance in long-distance transport.</p>
<p>India, which has just joined the very select club of heavy-haul railways, already operates dedicated freight corridors of over 3,000 kilometers and 13,000-tonne trains. Brazil follows the same path, but with an advantage: its corridors are born in territory of low railway density, where each new track reorganizes entire flows.</p>
<p>The soybeans leaving Água Boa, the corn coming down from Maracaju, the ore cutting through Vale do Aço — all this finds in the new corridors an alternative that the country abandoned for decades. FICO demonstrates that the institutional articulation among the regulatory agency, the planning state-owned company and the private concessionaire is capable of generating concrete and predictable works.</p>
<p>When the superstructure of Lot 1 is delivered in October, Brazil will have more than a physical landmark: it will have proof that transport planning can, finally, turn into track. Modernity, after all, has ballast.</p>
