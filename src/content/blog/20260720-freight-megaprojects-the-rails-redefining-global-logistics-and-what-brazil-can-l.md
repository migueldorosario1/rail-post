Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction. Railway megaprojects in countries like India, Turkey, Australia, and the United States are transforming global logistics, offering valuable lessons for Brazil.

Expanding railway logistics is fundamental for economic development, cost reduction, and emissions cuts. Countries that prioritize rail infrastructure are standing out on the global stage, with megaprojects underway in India, Turkey, Australia, and the United States, demonstrating the strategic importance of rails.

In India, the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor (EDFC) is under construction, with 1,200 km of dedicated tracks. The project quadrupled freight train capacity, from 32 to 130 train pairs per day. Although full completion is scheduled for 2026/2027, part of the corridor entered partial operation in 2023. Each 1 km freight train on the EDFC replaces 72 trucks, generating significant CO2 emission reductions.

In Turkey, the Marmaray, a completed railway project, connects the European and Asian sides of Istanbul, strengthening Turkey's position as a logistics hub on the modern Silk Road. The project diverts heavy traffic from Istanbul's city center, improving efficiency and the capacity to move large volumes. Half the route was built in tunnels, protecting operations from extreme heat, floods, and strong winds, ensuring continuous 24-hour operation.

In Australia, the Inland Rail, a 1,600 km project, will allow double-stacked container trains, replacing more than 100 trucks per train. The project, valued at around 31 billion Australian dollars, is compared by analysts to a Suez Canal on rails. The central idea is to move stacked containers across the interior, relieving congested highways. Each freight train can replace over a hundred trucks, cutting emissions and freeing roads for uses with lower economic impact, while meeting projected demand growth.

In the USA, the Pier B Rail Yard at the Port of Long Beach will receive US$283.4 million to expand yards and tracks, increasing on-rail container capacity by 20%. The St. Lucie River railroad bridge in Florida will be replaced by a double-track structure with larger clear spans, supporting Brightline passenger service. These investments aim to improve efficiency and the ability to move large volumes, crucial for economic development.

Rails represent a strategic choice for development. Countries building them today are defining their position on the economic map for the coming decades. According to the agency O Cafezinho, the future of global freight is built on rails. Sensors, dispatch algorithms, and electrified systems are turning railways into the foundation of a low-carbon economy. The energy efficiency of trains offers predictability that highways cannot replicate. Immense volumes move with reduced environmental impact, strengthening nations' logistical sovereignty.

In Brazil, where agribusiness still relies on trucks traveling thousands of kilometers, the message is direct. Without a dense and modern rail network, there is no sustainable competitiveness. The State must plan and execute these works as a national priority to break away from dependent and inefficient patterns. A promising example in Brazil is the megaproject by the Petrocity group, creating a railway complex linking the Brazilian Midwest to the port of São Mateus in Espírito Santo, cutting through 41 municipalities in Minas Gerais. The project, with a 10-year completion timeline, aims to serve mineral and steel production, as well as transport grains and ores destined for the international market.

Another large-scale project is the Central Bioceanic Corridor, which seeks to connect Mendoza, Argentina, to the Chilean coast of Valparaíso. The consortium formed by national and international companies envisions an estimated investment of 9.6 billion dollars. The project includes the construction of a railway tunnel, an electrified double-track railway, and a multimodal cargo center. According to its proponents, integrating these technologies seeks to consolidate a low-emission corridor that combines operational efficiency with environmental sustainability criteria.

The use of an electrified double-track line aims to reduce reliance on fossil fuels and lower the carbon footprint of freight transport between the two countries.
