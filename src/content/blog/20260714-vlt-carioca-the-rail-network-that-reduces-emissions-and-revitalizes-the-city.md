The VLT Carioca transforms urban mobility, reducing car usage and pollutant gas emissions. This project is an example of how infrastructure investments can promote sustainable development and quality of life in cities.

Every major city reveals its intelligence through the way it moves people. In Rio de Janeiro, the VLT Carioca is a concrete example of this statement, efficiently integrating different forms of transportation and promoting the reduction of CO2 emissions. The project, which has been operating for several years, has demonstrated its value in improving urban quality of life and promoting sustainable development.

The VLT Carioca has two lines covering 28 kilometers and currently transports about 65,000 people per day. The trams operate at frequencies of up to one train every three minutes between 6 AM and midnight, offering a reliable and efficient alternative for the city's residents and visitors. According to KfW, full operation is expected to avoid at least 5.6 million car trips per year, significantly contributing to the reduction of CO2 emissions.

With total investments of around 500 million euros, of which 133 million came from KfW in a loan through BNDES, the VLT Carioca stands out not only for its transport capacity but also for the innovative technology it uses. The Ground-Level Power Supply (APS) is a solution that eliminates the need for overhead wires, reducing visual pollution and increasing safety in urban areas. This technology allows the system to operate more cleanly and efficiently, aligning with global goals for reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

In 25 years, the VLT Carioca is expected to reduce over 300,000 tons of CO2, a significant impact on the global effort to mitigate planetary warming. Additionally, the project stimulates urban development in connected areas, generating economic opportunities and improving the quality of life for residents. The VLT Carioca connects the port area to the city center, passing through bus terminals, metro stations, airports, and other important points, creating an integrated and efficient network.

The integration of the VLT Carioca into Rio de Janeiro's transport network is not limited to mobility. The project also promotes urban revitalization, encouraging the development of previously neglected areas. The presence of the VLT has attracted new real estate and commercial investments, contributing to the revitalization of historic neighborhoods and the creation of local jobs. As Martin Kores, senior project manager at KfW, highlighted, "The climate impact was a clear focus in this project."

International experiences, such as those in Seville and Bilbao in Spain and Bordeaux in France, have shown that similar VLT systems can boost property occupancy and local economic development. In Brazil, the VLT Carioca serves as an inspiring model for other cities, showing that adopting modern and sustainable technologies can bring significant benefits for both mobility and the environment. The initiative is also aligned with the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 9, which aims to build resilient infrastructure, promote inclusive and sustainable industrialization, and foster innovation.

Furthermore, the VLT Carioca has the potential to transform urban dynamics, making the city center more attractive for residents, workers, and tourists. Modal integration, with connections to other means of transport such as the metro, buses, and bike lanes, promotes more sustainable travel and reduces dependence on individual motorized transport. Studies indicate that the adoption of unified ticketing and the construction of intermodal stations are fundamental strategies for the system's success.

The implementation of the VLT Carioca also has relevant economic impacts. The system tends to increase property values along its route, attract new investments, and energize local commerce, especially in under-occupied regions. The modal connection of tourist points can boost tourism in the center of Rio, increasing visitor flow and generating new jobs in the sector. Additionally, the VLT facilitates access for residents of peripheral areas to the city center, connecting to transport corridors and collective transport terminals, promoting social inclusion.

Based on data provided by <a href="https://www.kfw.de/stories/economy/mobility/tram-rio/" target="_blank">KfW</a>, the VLT Carioca is a successful example of how railway technology can be used to promote urban mobility, CO2 emission reduction, and sustainable development. The project demonstrates that with proper planning and strategic investments, it is possible to create transport solutions that benefit both the population and the environment. The VLT Carioca is, therefore, an important milestone in the history of urban mobility in Rio de Janeiro and an example to be followed by other Brazilian and international cities.
