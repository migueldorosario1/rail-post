While the country dreams of transcontinental railways and R$ 100 billion in freight concessions, the high-speed project reveals the abyssal gap between political ambition and technical reality.
'A high-speed train operates above 300 km/h and can reach even higher speeds in newer projects — the nature of the project, equipment, and components is completely different,' declares economist and president of Inter.B Consultoria Internacional de Negócios, Cláudio Frischtak. The statement is a death knell for the announcement that the São Paulo–Rio de Janeiro High-Speed Train (TAV), a Workers' Party campaign promise since 2007, <a href='https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/brasil/promessa-do-pt-obras-adiadas-trem-bala-sao-paulo-rio-de-janeiro/' target='_blank'>has had the start of works once again postponed</a>, now from 2027 to 2028.
The project reaches 18 years without a single rail laid. The new date pushes commercial operation to 2032, but the history of frustrations makes any schedule a piece of technical fiction.
TAV Brasil, the company that obtained authorization from the National Land Transport Agency (ANTT) based on the Railways Law (Law No. 14,273/2021), plans 417 kilometers of length and a maximum speed of 320 km/h. The route includes stations in São Paulo, São José dos Campos, Volta Redonda, and Rio de Janeiro, with a travel time of less than two hours.
The updated cost of the undertaking reaches R$ 60 billion, with projected fares between R$ 300 and R$ 500. Financing will be 100% private, but the project still lacks an EIA/RIMA — Ibama demanded the document in March 2024, and TAV Brasil has not submitted it.
While the TAV stalls, the federal government launched the <a href='https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/' target='_blank'>National Railway Plan with R$ 100 billion</a> and 4,700 kilometers of new lines, all for freight. Not a cent of this amount is allocated to a single kilometer of high-speed passenger rail.
The <a href='https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/08/ferrovia-para-ligar-o-atlantico-ao-pacifico-veja-o-que-se-sabe-ate-agora-do-projeto-do-brasil-com-a-china.ghtml' target='_blank'>Brazil-China agreement for a transcontinental railway</a>, signed in July 2025, aims to link the port of Ilhéus (BA) to the port of Chancay (Peru). Projections indicate that the rail-sea route would reduce Brazil-Asia transport time, an engineering and logistics feat that starkly contrasts with the high-speed train's paralysis.
The memorandum of understanding between Infra S.A. and the China Railway Economic Research and Planning Institute lasts five years and focuses on intermodality and sustainability. However, while one dreams of trains crossing South America, the country's densest passenger corridor remains hostage to empty promises.
Frischtak recalls that only China, France, Japan, and Spain dominate TAV technology over long distances. Spain, for example, leveraged its network with substantial transfers from the European Union, something without parallel in Brazil.
The economist points out that Brazil has never had construction experience with speeds beyond 80 km/h to 100 km/h, and the leap to 320 km/h represents an unprecedented technical and financial challenge.
