With 4,700 km of new tracks and auctions scheduled for 2026, the government bets on the railway network as a vector of competitiveness and continental integration.

The federal government is preparing the launch of the National Railway Plan, a project that projects R$ 100 billion in investments and the construction of 4,700 kilometers of new tracks. Tracks are the material geometry of the future, and Brazil seems determined to recover lost time in freight transport.

The central axis of the plan is the East-West Corridor, which will unify the West-East Integration Railway (Fiol) and the Center-West Integration Railway (Fico) into a 2,400-kilometer artery. Departing from Ilhéus, Bahia, the railway will reach Lucas do Rio Verde, Mato Grosso, connecting the agricultural frontier to the Atlantic coast.

Fiol is a public work fully financed by the Union via BNDES. Fico is a Union project with funds from the PAC (Growth Acceleration Program) and the federal budget, with no link to extensions of mining company Vale's concessions, as detailed by Transport Minister Renan Filho to CNN.

Another outflow vector is the extension of the North-South Railway by 477 kilometers, from Açailândia, Maranhão, to the port of Vila do Conde, Pará. Currently, grains arriving at the Maranhão terminal compete for space with ore from the Carajás Railway (EFC), operated with priority by Vale.

The Southeast Railway Ring, with 300 kilometers between Vitória (ES) and Itaboraí (RJ), promotes integration between the Vitória-Minas Railway (EFVM) and the MRS Logística network. This stitching eliminates historical bottlenecks in the Rio de Janeiro Metropolitan Region and creates a continuous corridor for commodity outflow.

The Transnordestina, promised since 2010 and now with delivery projected for 2026 or 2027, gains 600 kilometers of connection with the North-South in Estreito (MA). Meanwhile, Ferrogrão, with 933 kilometers between Sinop (MT) and Itaituba (PA), faces an impasse due to technical flaws and lack of adequate studies, leading IBAMA to suspend the environmental licensing in 2023. There is, however, no action in the Supreme Court (STF) on this matter.

To make these projects viable, the Union will assume 20% to 30% of the investments, funding that will come, in part, from renegotiation agreements with Rumo, MRS, and Vale. The EFVM and EFC concessions expire in 2031 and 2034, respectively, and no formalized, published, or confirmed agreement by the TCU (Federal Court of Accounts), ANTT (National Land Transport Agency), or the Ministry of Transport has been made as of March 2026.

The schedule provides for eight auctions in 2026, including the Minas Rio Corridor, the West Network, the South Network, and the East-West Corridor itself, according to a CNN Brasil report. Industry sources, however, admit that they will be satisfied if two of these bids actually reach B3 (the Brazilian stock exchange), which reveals the chronic distrust with railway promises.

In addition to the domestic market, the project dialogues with continental ambitions: in July 2025, Brazil and China signed a memorandum to study a transcontinental railway that would link the port of Ilhéus to the Peruvian port of Chancay. The objective is to shorten from 40 to 28 days the journey of Brazilian cargo to the Asian market, as reported by G1.

The scale of the National Railway Plan shows that the country is trying to overcome decades of logistical atrophy by betting on freight infrastructure and intermodal corridors. If the schedule resists the usual delays, Brazil may finally transform its tracks into real vectors of competitiveness and global insertion.
