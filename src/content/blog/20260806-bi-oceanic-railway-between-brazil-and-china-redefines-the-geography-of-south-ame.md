Reducing the continental crossing from 40 to 28 days is not just logistics — it is rewriting the productivity map between the Atlantic and the Pacific.

<p>Speed is also a form of civilization, and few infrastructures translate this maxim as clearly as freight railways. While Brazil still debates the cost per kilometer of rail — estimated at R$ 27 million, compared to R$ 8 million for a highway —, China advances with the logic of those who understand that the return is not measured only in transport tariffs, but in logistical sovereignty and territorial organization.</p>

<p>The agreement signed between Brazil and China to study a railway connecting the port of Ilhéus, in Bahia, to the port of Chancay, in Peru, is not a symbolic gesture.</p>

<p>According to the <a href="https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/08/ferrovia-para-ligar-o-atlantico-ao-pacifico-veja-o-que-se-sabe-ate-agora-do-projeto-do-brasil-com-a-china.ghtml" target="_blank">G1 report</a> on the details of the project, the route would cross Bahia, Goiás, Mato Grosso, Rondônia, and Acre before crossing the Peruvian border to the Pacific. It is an intervention that reorganizes the relative weight of the Southeast ports and shifts the outflow axis of Brazilian production towards a direct exit to Asia.</p>

<p>The mathematics of time explains the ambition: projections from the Peruvian government indicate that the journey from Brazil to China could drop from 40 to 28 days, shortening the logistics cycle for commodities by almost two weeks. For a country that shipped 104.4 million tons of agricultural products by rail in 2025 alone, each day saved represents freed-up working capital and regained competitiveness.</p>

<p>The project directly dialogues with the structure that China has already established on the South American Pacific — the port of Chancay, inaugurated in 2024 with Chinese funds as part of the New Silk Road. Brazil has not formally joined the program, but the memorandum of understanding signed between Infra S.A. and the China Railway Economic and Planning Research Institute lays bare that physical integration is already underway, regardless of diplomatic rites.</p>

<p>The National Secretary of Railway Transport, Leonardo Ribeiro, described the initiative as a 'strategic step' and a 'technical and diplomatic move to bring continents closer and reduce distances'. The statement echoes the recognition that Brazil missed the previous window — similar studies conducted between 2015 and 2016 were shelved due to lack of political maturity and scarcity of available infrastructure.</p>

<p>Today the scenario is different, but the challenges remain proportional to the scale of the work. The railway kilometer costs more than triple the highway kilometer, and the financial return can take decades to materialize — which is why, in practically all countries that expanded their networks, the state stepped in as guarantor or direct investor.</p>

<p>In China, the expansion was led by the state-owned China State Railway, while in Europe operators such as Deutsche Bahn and SNCF still sustain operations that do not pay for themselves solely through tickets or freight. Brazil, which has operated under a concession regime since the 1990s, is now testing a hybrid model in which the federal government covers 20% to 30% of investments to make projects viable.</p>

<p>The National Railway Plan, announced with a forecast of R$ 100 billion in investments and almost five thousand kilometers of new tracks, attempts to break the inertia with a pipeline that includes the East-West Corridor, the extension of the North-South railway to the port of Vila do Conde, the Southeast Railway Ring, and the Ferrogrão. The Ferrogrão, by the way, illustrates the type of friction that separates paper from gravel: designed to connect Sinop to Itaituba, it remained stuck for over a decade in environmental and judicial disputes until the Supreme Federal Court validated the reduction of the protection area of the Jamanxim National Park.</p>

<p>In parallel, the Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, is arranging a special line of financing at BNDES with terms of up to 60 years and extended grace period during construction — precisely the period when the railway generates no revenue. Financial engineering is inseparable from civil engineering in this type of undertaking, and the government is betting on revenues obtained from the renegotiation of old concessions to irrigate the new projects.</p>

<p>Chinese interest adds a geopolitical layer that transcends immediate economic viability. Companies such as CCCC, already present at the Port of São Luís, and CRRC, the world's largest train manufacturer and partner in the São Paulo Intercity Train, monitor the Brazilian portfolio with the patience of those playing continental chess.</p>

<p>Seventy percent of the soybeans and corn that China imports come from Brazil, and the state-owned Cofco already operates on the purchasing end of Brazilian farms — so that, for Beijing, financing rails in the Center-West is almost an extension of its own food security policy. The railway, in this calculation, may not yield a return as an isolated asset, but it reduces the final cost of the commodity that supplies the Chinese table.</p>

<p>Brazil transported 406.6 million tons of iron ore by rail in 2025, equivalent to 73% of all national railway cargo, and Vale operated 301 million tons of that total in a vertically integrated regime — mine, railway, and port under the same control. Agribusiness, although expanding, still represents a modest share of 19% of the volume transported, which highlights the room for growth if the network actually reaches the agricultural frontiers.</p>

<p>The difference between a railway that organizes the territory and a railway that merely extracts ore lies in the capacity to attract diverse cargoes, multiply intermodal terminals, and integrate regions that today depend on trucks as the only alternative. When the East-West Corridor connects Fico to Fiol along 1,700 kilometers, or when the North-South reaches Barcarena, what will be at stake is not just the ton transported, but the freight price that defines the viability of an entire harvest in the interior of the country.</p>

<p>By aiming for the Pacific, Brazil is not just shortening nautical distances — it is recognizing that productive modernity has a direction, and that rails are the most civilizing address that a continental country can offer to its own production.</p>

<p>The schedule of eight auctions planned for 2026 faces skepticism even among sector interlocutors, who consider it a victory if two undertakings actually reach B3. Even so, the technical and diplomatic movement now launched carries a rare quality: it treats the railway not as a campaign promise, but as the concrete bone of development.</p>
