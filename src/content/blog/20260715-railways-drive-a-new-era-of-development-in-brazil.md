Brazil is on the right track to modernize its railway infrastructure, with ambitious projects that promise to transform national and regional logistics, directly impacting the country's competitiveness in the international market.

Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction. In Brazil, the expansion and modernization of railways are becoming a concrete reality, with projects aimed at improving logistics, reducing costs, and promoting sustainable development.

The transcontinental railway, which will connect the port of Ilhéus in Bahia to the port of Chancay in Peru, is an emblematic example of this movement. According to the agreement signed between Brazil and China, technical studies are already underway to make this project viable, which promises to revolutionize the flow of Brazilian products to Asia.

The Brazilian government predicts that the construction of this transcontinental railway could reduce the transit time for cargo from Brazil to Asia from 40 to 28 days, as reported by the news portal <a href="https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/08/ferrovia-para-ligar-o-atlantico-ao-pacifico-veja-o-que-se-sabe-ate-agora-do-projeto-do-brasil-com-a-china.ghtml" target="_blank">G1</a>. This not only accelerates the logistics process but also reduces costs, making Brazil more competitive in the international market.

Furthermore, intermodality, which combines different modes such as railways, waterways, and highways, is a priority to ensure the efficiency and sustainability of the system. The National Railway Plan, which is awaiting final adjustments before launch, envisions an investment of approximately R$ 100 billion, involving almost five thousand kilometers of new railways.

This plan includes the concession of five major railway projects to the private sector, such as the East-West Corridor, the Extension of the North-South Railway, the Southeastern Railway Ring, the Transnordestina, and the Ferrogrão. These projects aim to strengthen the national railway network, facilitating the transport of grains, minerals, and other essential products.

The Union will cover part of the funding to ensure the economic viability of these ventures, as highlighted by the Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, in an interview with CNN Brasil. The Lula government has already closed agreements with companies such as Rumo and MRS Logística, and recently announced an understanding with Vale, which offered to pay up to R$ 17 billion for the extension of the EFC and EFVM concessions.

In the Central-West region, the National Land Transport Agency (ANTT) authorized the construction of a 47-kilometer railway branch, which will connect the future Arauco factory to the national railway network. This project, which is the first railway authorization granted by ANTT to enter the construction phase, aims to boost logistics and reduce the number of trucks on highways, thus promoting greater efficiency and safety.

The estimated annual production of 3.5 million tons of cellulose will be transported by rail to Rumo's Northern Network and from there to the Port of Santos (SP), destined for the United States, Europe, and Asia. This project not only boosts the industrial sector but also generates jobs and contributes to regional development, as emphasized by the General Director of ANTT, Guilherme Theo Sampaio.

Additionally, VLI, the company operating the Centro-Atlântica Railway (FCA), invested R$ 700 million in the acquisition of eight new locomotives, specially designed for Brazilian railways. These locomotives, purchased from Progress Rail, a Caterpillar group company, will operate on the FCA, serving essential sectors such as agribusiness and steelmaking.

With greater load capacity, enhanced operational safety, and lower environmental impact, these locomotives bring significant benefits to the production chain and society. The federal government also plans to accelerate the railway agenda in 2026, with eight projects involving concessions, early renewals, re-tendering, and public calls.

Although there are some challenges, such as the need to update studies and obtain regulatory approvals, the expectation is that 2026 will mark an operational turnaround for the sector, after years of revisions and uncertainties. The modernization and expansion of railways in Brazil are not just about infrastructure; they represent a strategic step for the socioeconomic development of the country.

With massive investments, international partnerships, and a forward-looking vision, Brazil is preparing to face logistical and environmental challenges, ensuring more efficient and sustainable logistics that will benefit both the economy and the quality of life of citizens.
