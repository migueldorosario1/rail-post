The dream of the TAV contrasts with the reality of Brazilian rails, where the logistical urgency of commodities and China's financing redraw the national railway map.

Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction.
Few projects symbolize the mismatch between the developmentalist dream and the Brazilian fiscal reality as well as the Rio-São Paulo High-Speed Train (TAV). While TAV Brasil, the company holding the authorization to build and operate the railway axis, postpones the start of works to 2028, the Chinese locomotive silently advances over the freight and medium-speed rails in the country.
The new postponement of the bullet train, reported exclusively by <a href="https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/brasil/promessa-do-pt-obras-adiadas-trem-bala-sao-paulo-rio-de-janeiro/" target="_blank">Gazeta do Povo</a>, exposes a drastic shift in stance from the Planalto Palace. The government of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva now states that the R$ 60 billion project "is not the government's", discarding the direct contribution of Union resources that had been floated in 2023 by Transport Minister Renan Filho.
The 417-kilometer route envisions a trip in less than two hours, with a maximum speed of 320 km/h. To give an idea of the scale of the challenge, the average cost of implementing a railway in Brazil is around R$ 27 million per kilometer, more than three times the value of a highway, as sector specialists point out.
The TAV Brasil project foresees stations in the São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro capitals, as well as São José dos Campos (SP) and Volta Redonda (RJ). However, the Brazilian Institute of the Environment and Renewable Natural Resources (Ibama) still demands the presentation of the Environmental Impact Study and Environmental Impact Report (EIA/Rima), a document without which the overhead catenary and interventions in the Serra do Mar remain merely in the realm of intentions.
The financial engineering has also changed shape since the PT embraced the TAV banner in the Growth Acceleration Program (PAC) in 2007. Under the aegis of the Railways Law (Law 14.273/2021), sanctioned in the previous government, the current model dispenses with the traditional concession and relies entirely on private authorization, without a single cent of confirmed state subsidy.
While the TAV stalls, the Chinese presence materializes in much more down-to-earth projects. CRRC, the world's largest rolling stock manufacturer, is a partner of Grupo Comporte in the concession of the Intercity Train that will connect São Paulo to Campinas, a venture budgeted at R$ 14 billion.
To consolidate this technological bridge, the Chinese state-owned company is preparing a factory in Araraquara, in the interior of São Paulo state. The unit, with an initial planned investment of R$ 50 million, is born with the strategic mission of producing trains for the São Paulo Metro and for the Intercity Train itself, anchoring the supply chain on Brazilian soil.
Chinese capital is not only interested in urban mobility. The appetite for structuring freight projects is revealed in the memorandum of understanding signed in July 2025 between Infra S.A. and the Planning and Economic Research Institute of China Railway, as reported by <a href="https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/07/brasil-e-china-assinam-memorando-para-planejamento-ferroviario.ghtml" target="_blank">G1</a>.
The plan is ambitious: to transport the production of the Center-West and Matopiba to the Port of Chancay, in Peru, shortening the maritime route to Asia by 12 days. The National Secretary of Railway Transport, Leonardo Ribeiro, described the partnership as “the first step of a technical and diplomatic journey to bring continents closer”.
This logistical pragmatism contrasts with the financial complexity of the TAV. Economist Cláudio Frischtak, president of Inter.B Consultoria, is categorical in stating that “it is an extremely expensive project and very difficult to justify from an economic point of view”, emphasizing that only China, France, Japan, and Spain master this technology on a large scale.
The geopolitical reality shows that Beijing prefers to land its investments where demand is guaranteed. Not by chance, five Chinese companies are already studying infrastructure projects in the country, targeting the eight railway auctions planned by the Ministry of Transport for 2026, which total R$ 140 billion in investments and 9 thousand kilometers in length.
The National Railway Plan, announced with pomp by the government and an investment of R$ 100 billion, details a pipeline of projects that includes Ferrogrão, the East-West Corridor, and the extension of the North-South, as reported by <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>. These are corridors designed exclusively for freight, connecting mines and farms to the ports of the Northern Arc and the Southeast.
While the new concessions matrix advances, the dream of high-speed rail remains hostage to a paradox: Brazil, which carried 406.6 million tons of iron ore on its lines in 2025, has never built a single kilometer of railway designed exclusively for passengers at 300 km/h. Modernity, after all, is not imposed by decree or campaign promise.
It demands political ballast, institutional gauge, and a current of investments that today clearly points to the East.
