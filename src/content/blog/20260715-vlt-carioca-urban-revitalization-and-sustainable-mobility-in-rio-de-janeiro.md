The Light Rail Vehicle (LRV) is an innovative solution for urban mobility, promoting revitalization and improving the quality of life for residents. Every major city reveals its intelligence by how it moves people, and the VLT Carioca is a notable example of this.

The Light Rail Vehicle (LRV) has stood out as a sustainable and integrated alternative for urban transport, transforming the landscape and dynamics of cities. In Rio de Janeiro, the VLT Carioca is already in operation, demonstrating its effectiveness and positive impact. Every major city reveals its intelligence by how it moves people, and the VLT Carioca is a notable example of this, providing a modern and efficient experience for users.

The VLT Carioca, with 28 km of track, connects various strategic points in the city, including bus stations, metro, ferries, downtown Rio, and Santos Dumont Airport, totaling 46 stations and stops. This public transport system not only facilitates mobility but also integrates different modes of transport, making the city more accessible and connected. According to the City of Rio de Janeiro, the project is part of the Porto Maravilha Urban Operation, which aims to revitalize the port and central areas of the city.

Each car of the VLT Carioca travels at an average speed of 17 km/h and can carry up to 420 passengers, offering comfort and efficiency. The payment system is through voluntary validation, integrated with the Single Ticket, facilitating the transition between different means of transport. With the capacity to reach up to 300,000 passengers per day, the VLT Carioca is a robust solution for urban mobility demand. The implementation of the VLT Carioca provides the population and visitors with an innovative and modern urban mobility alternative in the Center and Port Area, regions with a strong tourism (cultural and gastronomic) vocation, as well as creating a system that integrates the financial center and the cultural corridor with other modes of transport.

One of the most innovative technical aspects of the VLT Carioca is the absence of overhead wires or catenaries for capturing electrical energy. Energy supply is provided through the Ground-Level Power Supply (GLPS) system, which uses underground powerboxes to feed the vehicles. This technology avoids visual pollution caused by overhead wires and reduces the risk of electrical accidents. Additionally, each vehicle has energy banks, allowing it to continue operating even in case of failures in the powerboxes, ensuring the continuity of service.

The construction and operation of the VLT Carioca were carried out through a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) between the City of Rio de Janeiro and the responsible concessionaire. This management model allows for the combination of resources and expertise from the public and private sectors, resulting in a more efficient and sustainable project. According to economist Josiane Almeida, representative of the New Business Directorate of the Companhia de Concessões Rodoviárias (CCR), the initial project aimed at the reurbanization of the port area, focusing on the recovery of public spaces and improvement of infrastructure.

Urban revitalization is one of the main benefits of the VLT Carioca. The presence of this mode of transport stimulates the revitalization of previously degraded areas, promoting real estate appreciation and the creation of new leisure and social spaces. As explained by engineer Tiago Augusto Alves, also from CCR, the VLT Carioca is not just a means of transport, but a cultural change, encouraging the adoption of sustainable solutions and harmonious coexistence with other modes of transportation.

Furthermore, the VLT Carioca significantly contributes to the reduction of atmospheric and noise pollution, improving the quality of life for residents and visitors. The use of electric energy and the absence of polluting emissions make the VLT an environmentally friendly option, aligned with global carbon reduction goals and the promotion of more sustainable cities.

The success of the VLT Carioca serves as an inspiring example for other Brazilian and international cities. Similar projects are being planned and implemented in various locations, such as São Paulo, where the Bonde São Paulo seeks to reorganize the mobility of the Center, prioritizing road safety, accessibility, and increasing green areas. These projects share the common goal of providing a clean, efficient, and integrated transport alternative, contributing to the formation of more human and welcoming urban environments.

In summary, the VLT Carioca represents a milestone in the transformation of urban mobility, combining advanced technology, sustainability, and urban revitalization. Its implementation in Rio de Janeiro demonstrates that with proper planning and strategic investments, it is possible to create transport systems that benefit both the population and the environment, enhancing the quality of life and competitiveness of cities.
