Speed is also a form of civilization, and the new rail system in the capital of Bahia stitches together previously neglected territories into a single mobility network.

Speed is also a form of civilization, and Salvador is beginning to prove this maxim with the implementation of its Light Rail Vehicle (VLT). The largest urban intervention ever carried out in the Subúrbio Ferroviário region not only replaces the old train but stitches the city together based on a modern concept of integrated mobility.

With an estimated investment of R$5.4 billion, the project transcends the mere function of transportation to become a vector for urban and environmental requalification. The new network, about 43.7 kilometers long, more than triples the 13.5 kilometers of the railway deactivated in 2021, according to a report by Bahia Econômica<a href="https://bahiaeconomica.com.br/wp/2026/07/22/nos-trilhos-do-futuro-como-o-vlt-redesenha-a-mobilidade-de-areas-historicamente-negligenciadas-de-salvador/" target="_blank">.</a>

The backbone of the project lies in creating a structural transportation corridor that breaks the historical isolation of the neighborhoods from Calçada to Paripe. Each VLT train set will have seven cars and a capacity for about 400 passengers, operating in a system divided into three strategic lots covering from the suburban core to the waterfront of Piatã.

The technical logic is that of physical and fare integration. In Águas Claras, the VLT will connect to the metro, the bus terminal, and urban bus lines, while at São Joaquim Terminal integration will occur with the ferry-boat waterway system, forming the embryo of the new Integrated Metropolitan System (SIM).

The engineering of the project reflects a maturing in national mobility planning, by treating each mode as a link in the same travel chain. The projected demand, which should range between 100,000 and 150,000 users per day when the entire operation is completed, shows the scale of the expected impact on the urban network.

The integration of systems such as metro, VLT, and buses in exclusive corridors is the standard that cities like Manila are pursuing by approving mega transfer centers to break the fragmentation of commutes. The approval of the Unified Central Station in the Philippine capital, which will connect four railway lines under one roof, exactly reflects the logic applied in the Águas Claras corridor, where before passengers needed to cover long stretches on foot or by auxiliary vehicles to make the transfer, as highlighted by Gulf News<a href="https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/philippines/4-rail-lines-1-transfer-manila-finally-clears-mega-transit-hub-that-could-transform-commuting-in-the-philippine-capital-1.500593569" target="_blank">.</a>

The first stretch of Salvador's VLT, Lot 1, has already reached about 75% completion and is expected to be finished in 2027, with assisted operation underway since June between Calçada and Lobato. The gradual operation, without fare collection in this preliminary phase, allows calibration of the signaling and power supply systems before the full commercial opening.

In parallel, the scope of Lot 2 advances beyond 50% completion, focusing on the crossing of Estrada do Derba and the connection of tracks towards the Atlantic waterfront. Lot 3, in turn, awaits the service order to expand the frontier of rail transport towards new centralities in the Metropolitan Region.

The technical substrate of the project does not ignore the complexity of the topography and the pre-existing urban fabric. Macro-drainage works, slope containment, and recovery of approximately three kilometers of mangroves in the stretch between Lobato and Enseada do Cabrito are part of the infrastructure package, repositioning the environmental asset as a component of the mobility system.

This approach of linking mass transit with the recovery of degraded areas and the creation of linear parks reveals a vision of urban engineering that does not segregate the rail alignment from the social fabric that surrounds it. The construction of Salvador Skatepark in Praia Grande, with international certification from World Skate, is a byproduct of this logic, which injects leisure facilities into historically neglected regions.

The Ground-Level Power Supply (APS) system, discussed in projects such as the underground VLT in Brasília for Avenida W3, represents the pursuit of integrating aesthetics and energy efficiency in new corridors, eliminating the visual pollution of overhead catenary networks. Although distinct in their underground implementation, Brazilian projects share the goal of transforming this mode into a driver of densification and revitalization, as reported by Olhar da Notícia<a href="https://olhardanoticia.com.br/vlt-subterraneo-brasilia-tecnologia-aps-mobilidade/" target="_blank">.</a>

The breadth of Salvador's VLT, however, is of unique capillarity, with 50 stops distributed along the three sections that reorganize the circulation of entire neighborhoods. The expectation of the Bahia State Transport Company (CTB) is that, by the end of 2028, the network will be connected to the Piatã region, extending the reach of mass transit to the northern shoreline of the capital.

There are ongoing studies to take the rails to Ribeira, in the Lower City, and to connect Baixa do Fiscal to the Retiro metro station, passing through Largo do Tanque and San Martin, closing a reticular network that rationalizes metropolitan mobility. The consolidation of the SIM Functional Project, a methodology already applied in other metropolises such as Baixada Santista, demonstrates that the reorganization of the public transportation network becomes more efficient when anchored in a high-capacity trunk axis.

In the São Paulo experience, SYSTRA Brasil consolidated the alignment guideline and intermodal integration studies for the Barreiros-Porto VLT, showing that technical precision in defining the rolling stock and reorganizing the road system is what ensures the functionality of the integrated system. In Salvador, the same rigor is required to transform the old railway bed into a civilizing axis that reduces dependence on long trips by bus.

The replacement of a mode that carried less than 1% of public transport passengers by a system projecting up to 150,000 daily users redefines the hierarchy of mass transit in the capital of Bahia. The expropriation interventions necessary to make way for the new rail lines, although painful for part of the commerce and local population, occur in a context of transformation that restores to Subúrbio its original vocation as a space connected to the city's economic dynamic.

The assisted operation already underway anticipates the future, while the machines continue to shape the rest of the route. Salvador demonstrates, with the VLT, that modern urban mobility is the result of the inseparable stitching together of transport engineering, urban planning, and fare integration, proving that speed on rails is, in fact, a concrete instrument of civilization.
