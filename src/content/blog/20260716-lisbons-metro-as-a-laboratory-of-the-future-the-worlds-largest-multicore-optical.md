LUMIRing project uses 26 km of the Yellow Line to multiply data transmission capacity and places Portugal at the forefront of next-generation optical communications.

Rails are the material geometry of the future. In the Lisbon underground, this geometry has just gained a new layer of intelligence: the Metro network has become the world's largest multicore optical fiber test bed. The project is called LUMIRing — Lisbon Underground Multicore Fiber Ring — and turns 26 kilometers of the Yellow Line, between Odivelas and Rato, into a living laboratory for next-generation optical communications.

According to a statement from <a href="https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/2025/11/03/metropolitano-de-lisboa-e-iscte-inauguram-maior-banco-de-testes-de-fibra-otica-multinucleo-do-mundo-3/" target="_blank">Metropolitano de Lisboa</a>, more than 1,900 kilometers of multicore fiber were installed, of which 728 kilometers are of the same type. This unprecedented infrastructure uses existing tunnels and galleries to test, in a real urban environment, fibers with four and seven cores capable of multiplying data transmission capacity. Instead of a single core guiding light, each fiber carries multiple simultaneous beams, as if several channels were flowing in parallel within the same glass cylinder.

The practical difference is huge and explains the interest from global operators. The Vice-Rector of Iscte – University Institute of Lisbon, Jorge Costa, stated that the terrestrial test bed turns Portugal into a global center for optical telecommunications. He revealed that US-based AT&T, Deutsche Telekom, and Vodafone are already in the investment phase to increase the capacity of their networks and look to Lisbon as a technology validation platform.

The technical arrangement of LUMIRing is not only extensive but also collaborative and multinational. Germany's Heraeus Covantics supplied the special optical glass, Italy's Tratos Cavi produced the cables, and Japan's National Institute of Information and Communications Technology (NICT) has partnered for tests throughout 2026. The project's scientific lead, Adolfo Cartaxo, emphasizes that the collaboration with NICT proves the international interest in the realistic underground environment that the Lisbon metro offers.

For Metropolitano de Lisboa, the project goes beyond scientific prestige. Acting President Maria Helena Campos described the initiative as a unique opportunity to test cutting-edge technologies at a real operational scale. She stressed that the partnership highlights the company's ability to attract projects of high scientific value, projecting its image internationally and fostering synergies with academic institutions.

The gains for the transport system itself are not negligible. Multicore fibers could, in the future, support much denser digitalization of the metro network's control, communication, and safety systems. Intelligent operation management, based on massive real-time data, depends precisely on this kind of leap in transmission capacity. What is today a test bed for global telecommunications could tomorrow become the metro's own digital nervous system, enhancing energy efficiency and operational resilience.

Although LUMIRing is an exclusively Portuguese project, multicore optical fiber technology knows no borders. Rapidly expanding metro systems — such as those in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, or Belo Horizonte — could, in the future, replicate this living laboratory model using their own tunnels and galleries. Installing multiple fibers during expansion or modernization works would represent a leap in infrastructural intelligence, combining passenger transport with an ultra-high-capacity communications backbone.

Lisbon demonstrates that mobility infrastructure can be, at the same time, a platform for scientific experimentation and a strategic asset of digital sovereignty. Rails, after all, do not only move trains: they move data, ideas, and future possibilities. The material geometry we speak of is not a metaphor; it is real engineering 30 meters underground, where light travels multiple paths within a single glass thread and reinvents what it means to be connected.
