Brazil faces the challenge of modernizing its public transportation system, focusing on metros, LRVs, and BRTs, to meet growing urban demand. Strategic investments and careful planning are essential to transform urban mobility and promote a more sustainable and efficient future.

Modernity is not abstract. It has foundation, gauge, energy, and direction. In Brazil, the expansion and modernization of public transportation, especially through metros, Light Rail Vehicles (LRVs), and Bus Rapid Transit (BRTs), are essential to boost urban development and the quality of life for citizens. A recent study by the National Bank for Economic and Social Development (BNDES) highlights that the country needs to double the metro network and quadruple the BRT and LRV networks by 2054, with estimated investments of R$ 500 billion.

The Brazilian government plans to expand the metro network by 323 km, while the BRT and LRV network should grow from 631 km to 2,500 km. These numbers reflect the urgent need to improve public transportation infrastructure, which has suffered a 43% drop in the number of passengers since 2014, mainly due to migration to individual means of transportation.

According to Felipe Borim, Superintendent of the Infrastructure Area at BNDES, resource mobilization is crucial to face the scarcity and difficulty in implementing these projects. In a recent meeting, Borim highlighted that cheaper solutions, such as BRT, have proven important, although less efficient than rail transport. The implementation cost of BRT is around R$ 50 million per kilometer, while the metro can reach up to R$ 1 billion per kilometer.

The National Railway Plan, which depends only on final adjustments to be launched, foresees the concession of five major railway projects to the private sector, totaling almost 5,000 kilometers of new railways and an estimated investment of approximately R$ 100 billion. The plan was presented to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT) by the Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, who expects the initiative to be launched in February.

The project includes the expansion of the West-East Integration Railway (FIOL) and the Central-West Integration Railway (FICO), the extension of the North-South Railway, the Southeastern Railway Ring, the Transnordestina, and the Ferrogrão. These projects aim to improve national logistics, reduce truck traffic on highways, and promote Brazil's competitiveness in the international market.

In the state of São Paulo, the government announced an investment of R$ 57 billion to expand the metro network by about 50 km, plus R$ 14 billion for 22 km of railway network. The schedule involves simultaneous works on lines 2-Green, 4-Yellow, 6-Orange, 15-Silver, 17-Gold, 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire, and 13-Jade. The project, which will result in 72 additional kilometers of rail transport, includes the unprecedented extension of Line 4-Yellow outside the capital, something never before done in the system's history.

Line 2-Green of the São Paulo Metro, known as the 'Paulista Line' for passing through Paulista Avenue, is being expanded to Penha (East Zone). Meanwhile, Line 15-Silver, a monorail, connects the East Zone to Vila Prudente, with future expansions planned for Ipiranga, Boa Esperança, and Jacu Pêssego. Line 6-Orange, a new underground line under construction, will connect the North Zone (Brasilândia) to the Center (São Joaquim) and is scheduled to start partial operation in October 2026 and full operation in 2027.

Governor Tarcísio de Freitas (Republicans) promulgated, on Monday (15th), the law authorizing the State to contract loans of up to US$ 425 million, destined for the expansion of Lines 2-Green and 6-Orange of the metro. The expansion project covers neighborhoods in the expanded center and the east zone of the capital, such as Aclimação, Mooca, and Penha, and also envisages extending operations to the municipality of Guarulhos.

The National Land Transport Agency (ANTT) also plays a fundamental role in this process. On Friday (6/2), in Inocência (MS), ANTT General Director Guilherme Theo Sampaio participated in the groundbreaking ceremony for a new railway section, the first railway authorization granted by ANTT that is actually entering the construction phase. With 47 kilometers in length, the short-line railway branch will connect the future Arauco factory, which will be the world's largest cellulose industry, to the national railway network.

This new phase of Brazilian railways gains strength in the Southeast. On the next Monday (9/2), in Sete Lagoas (MG), an event will be held to deliver eight new locomotives acquired by VLI from Progress Rail, a Caterpillar group company. The total investment reaches R$ 700 million, including acquisition and maintenance contracts. The locomotives, specially designed for Brazilian railways, will operate on the Central-Atlantic Railway (FCA), serving essential sectors such as agribusiness and steelmaking.

The presence of ANTT in these two milestones, the start of railway construction in the Midwest and the strengthening of the railway fleet in the Southeast, simply and directly translates the Agency's role: to regulate, supervise, and enable solutions that impact people's real lives, promoting the socioeconomic development of the country. This work ensures legal security, regulatory balance, and focus on the public interest, allowing ANTT to help Brazil advance responsibly, predictably, and with a vision for the future.

Infrastructure is not just tracks, locomotives, or construction. It is development that arrives, jobs that are created, products that circulate, and quality of life that improves. 'Because, in the end, infrastructure is made by people, for people, and it is in this way that Brazil continues to advance, literally, on the tracks of development,' concluded ANTT General Director Guilherme Theo Sampaio.

The BNDES study mapped 194 medium or high-capacity public collective transportation projects and pointed out that Brazil needs to double the size of the metro system and quadruple the BRT and LRV systems in the coming decades. The plan is being developed with the Ministry of Cities and envisages investments of up to R$ 500 billion until 2054—thirty years after the survey began. According to the survey, Brazil's base metro network (already built and what is in the implementation phase) reaches 376 kilometers. The planned increase is an additional 323 kilometers. The BRT, LRV, or monorail network—categories aggregated in the study—would grow from 631 to 2,500 kilometers.

The National Association of Urban Transport Companies cites that buses have seen a 44.1% drop in the number of passengers transported over the past ten years. This equates to about 19.1 million fewer trips per day between 2014 and 2023. During the pandemic, demand fell by up to 80% at its peak in March 2020. By mid-2023, the total number of users remained about 15% below pre-pandemic levels, indicating lost demand that migrated to other modes, such as personal cars, motorcycles, or ride-sharing apps.

The number of people served by high and medium-capacity modes could increase by up to 501%—as in the case of Fortaleza—if the future network is effectively implemented, with an optimized scenario being constructed. Today, the capital of Ceará serves 213,000 people in these modes but would jump to 1.2 million. This more optimistic scenario depends on four factors: implementing a fare that commits no more than 6% of the average worker's income; integrating different modes into a single or reduced fare; policies to discourage car use, such as restricting circulation in certain areas and reducing parking spaces; and prioritizing these collective transports in traffic with priority signaling and reduction of retention points, for example.

With this, the medium or high-capacity public collective transportation in Rio, for example, could go from 1.6 million users to 5.9 million—an increase of 270%. In São Paulo, the increase would be 68%, from 8.9 million to 15 million people served. The projects have various degrees of maturity, and according to Borim, the expectation is that the final list will be published in a few months. 'The projects have various degrees of maturity. We may find some more mature ones and potentially start construction next year. Only after this analysis is complete can we say this with certainty,' clarifies the BNDES representative.

The challenge of modernizing and expanding the public transportation system in Brazil is complex but essential for urban development and the quality of life of citizens. With strategic investments and careful planning, the country can make significant progress in the coming years, transforming urban mobility and promoting a more sustainable and efficient future.
