The transformation of the Lisbon Metro into an advanced optical fiber testing center positions the city at the forefront of global innovation, demonstrating the potential of rail transport as a vector for technological development.

Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction. In Lisbon, Portugal, this statement comes to concrete life with the inauguration of the Lisbon Underground Multicore Fiber Ring – LUMIRing, the world's largest multicore optical fiber testbed. The project, a collaboration between the Lisbon Metro, ISCTE, Heraeus Covantics of Germany, and Tratos Cavi of Italy, transforms the Lisbon Metro's underground network into a "living laboratory" of technological innovation.

The system spans 26 kilometers of underground infrastructure, making over 1,900 km of multicore optical fiber available for testing. This fiber, with four and seven cores, promises to multiply data transmission capacity and revolutionize global communications from 2026. LUMIRing is installed along the Yellow Line, between Odivelas and Rato, creating a unique environment to test and validate new communication solutions under real conditions.

The initiative places the Lisbon Metro at the heart of national technological innovation, offering exceptional conditions to experiment with next-generation optical fibers. According to Maria Helena Campos, Acting President of the Lisbon Metro, "this collaboration represents a unique opportunity to test cutting-edge technologies in a real urban environment with operational scale". The project highlights the Lisbon Metro's ability to attract projects of high scientific and technological value, projecting its image on the national and international stage.

Jorge Costa, ISCTE's Vice-Rector for Research and Technological Modernization, emphasizes that LUMIRing "will transform Portugal into a world center for optical telecommunications, attracting investment in communication technologies, Artificial Intelligence, and digital transformation". The interest has already caught the attention of global operators such as the American AT&T, Deutsche Telekom, and Vodafone, which are in the investment phase to increase the capacity of their networks.

Adolfo Cartaxo, the project's scientific lead, adds that the collaboration agreed with Japan's National Institute of Information and Communication Technology (NICT) to carry out tests and demonstrations of large-scale multicore fiber systems throughout 2026 is a concrete example of this interest in LUMIRing. This underground ring of innovation, traversing tunnels and stations of the metro's Yellow Line, positions Lisbon as a global technological benchmark.

The impact of LUMIRing goes beyond science. As ISCTE's rector, Maria de Lurdes Rodrigues, reports, "we created a unique laboratory, not only in Portugal but also in Europe. It is a field that requires extremely high levels of specialization, but that generates results with global impact". The project promises to positively influence society, generating highly qualified jobs and attracting talent, companies, and international investment to the region.

Installed beneath our feet, but with borderless impacts, LUMIRing is a technological breakthrough and a shared vision for the future of the city and the country. By bringing together academia, the public sector, and industry in a collaborative ecosystem, the project lays the foundations for a more efficient and resilient urban future, transforming the Portuguese capital into a true model of innovation and global progress.

Although the project is centered in Lisbon, it offers valuable lessons for other cities, including Brazil. The integration of advanced technologies into transport systems can boost innovation, energy efficiency, and sustainability, contributing to the development of smart and resilient cities. The Lisbon example demonstrates that rail transport can be much more than mere means of mobility; they can be vectors of technological and social transformation.

The Lisbon Metro, with LUMIRing, reaffirms its centrality in the mobility ecosystem of the Metropolitan Area, consolidating itself as a modern and innovative operator. By placing technology at the service of mobility and urban progress, the Lisbon Metro reinforces its structuring role in building smart and resilient cities, aligned with the principles of digital transformation and sustainable development.

With the support of the Lisbon 2030 Regional Programme – Technological Infrastructures, the project received an investment of €1.4 million, with approximately €588,000 from the ERDF fund. This investment represents much more than a technological advance — it is a catalyst for strategic development for the capital region. By positioning Lisbon at the forefront of experimentation in optical communications, the project strengthens the city's capacity to attract talent, companies, and international investment, consolidating it as a hub of scientific and technological excellence.

The transformation of the Lisbon Metro into a living laboratory of technological innovation illustrates the potential of rail transport as vectors of development. LUMIRing is not just an engineering project, but a declaration of intent: that modernity, with its ballast, gauge, energy, and direction, is ready to change the world, starting with the underground tracks of Lisbon.
