Cargo, a central element in railway development, is the vector that drives logistical sovereignty, territorial transformation, and the geopolitical reconfiguration of Brazil. Focusing on Fiol, the structural axis of the East-West Corridor, we explore the strategic importance of the railway network for the country's future.

Speed is also a form of civilization. In this perspective, the expansion of railways in Brazil emerges as a fundamental catalyst for the modernization and economic development of the country. The East-West Corridor, composed of the West-East Integration Railway (Fiol) and the Central-West Integration Railway (Fico), totaling about 2,400 km, stands out as an emblematic project in this context.

Fiol, originating in Ilhéus, Bahia, already has granted and under-construction sections. Approximately 68% of Fiol 2 is completed, with projections to reach 71% after new works published in March 2026, according to information from the Ministry of Transport. The intermodal corridor, which connects the productive Central-West to the port of Ilhéus, not only facilitates the flow of products but also redefines the country's economic geography.

Each kilometer of railway in Brazil costs, on average, R$ 27 million—more than three times the cost per km of highway (R$ 8 million). Despite this high initial investment, the drainage capacity and operational efficiency of railways justify the expense. This is especially true when considering the logistics verticalization and the integration of different modes of transport.

In 2025, 73% of all Brazilian railway cargo (406.6 million tons) was iron ore, mostly operated by Vale. Grains represented 19% (104.4 million tons). These figures illustrate the strategic importance of railways for the productive sector, highlighting the need to expand the network for other types of cargo and regions.

Ferrogrão, with 933 km between Sinop and Itaituba, had its judicial advancement released in April 2026, following a decision by the Supreme Court (STF) validating the reduction of protected area in the Jamanxim National Park. This project, in addition to being crucial for agricultural drainage, demonstrates the complexity involved in implementing railway infrastructure, balancing economic and environmental interests.

The convergence of the East-West Corridor with the future transcontinental Brazil-Peru railway, which will connect the port of Ilhéus to the port of Chancay, on the Peruvian coast, represents an unprecedented opportunity for Brazil. According to the national secretary of Railway Transport, Leonardo Ribeiro, the initiative is a 'strategic step' and a 'technical and diplomatic move to bring continents closer and reduce distances'.

This project, led by Infra S.A. and the Ministry of Transport, aims to facilitate the drainage of Brazilian products to Asia, especially to China, Brazil's main trading partner. Although initial studies are ongoing, the expectation is that the new route could reduce the average transportation time of cargo from Brazil to Asia from 40 to 28 days, as indicated by the Peruvian government.

However, the expansion of the railway network in Brazil faces significant challenges. In addition to the high implementation cost, the economic viability of railway projects depends on a set of factors, including financing, market demand, and governmental support.

The National Railway Plan, presented by the Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, foresees the concession of five major railway projects to the private sector, with estimated investments of approximately R$ 100 billion. Among these projects, the Southeast Railway Ring, the extension of the North-South Railway, and the Transnordestina are examples of ventures aimed at improving the connectivity and logistical efficiency of the country.

To make these projects viable, the government relies on resources raised from the renegotiation of concessions in the sector. The contracts were extended during the management of former President Jair Bolsonaro but were renegotiated in the current administration. The Lula government has already closed agreements with companies such as Rumo and MRS Logística, and announced an understanding with Vale, which agreed to pay up to R$ 17 billion for the extension of the EFC and EFVM concessions.

These agreements are crucial to ensure the continuity and expansion of railway operations, allowing the sector to maintain its competitiveness and contribute to the country's economic development. International participation, especially Chinese, is also essential for the success of these projects.

Chinese companies, such as CCCC and CRRC, have already shown interest in participating in railway ventures in Brazil. For example, CCCC already has a stake in the São Luís Port, in Maranhão, and has expressed interest in the Fico-Fiol corridor. CRRC, the world's largest train manufacturer, is present in Brazil through partnerships and is preparing a factory in Araraquara, in the interior of São Paulo, to produce trains for the São Paulo Metro and the Intercity Train.

These international partnerships not only bring capital and technology but also strengthen Brazil's commercial and diplomatic relations, expanding its options for access to global markets. The federal government's railway agenda, which foresees eight auctions in 2026, already faces some challenges. The first project, the Minas Rio Corridor, which was scheduled to go to auction on April 26, had its notice postponed to May, with the public call expected in August.

Despite these obstacles, the strategy combines different instruments—renewal, concession, re-tendering, and authorizations—in an attempt to unlock investments and reorganize the existing network. The internal assessment is that, after years of revisions in the railway plan and uncertainties about the sector, 2026 may mark an operational turnaround for the sector.

The expansion of railways in Brazil, focusing on the East-West Corridor and Fiol, is not just an infrastructure issue but also a matter of logistical sovereignty and territorial transformation. Cargo, as both a material and symbolic protagonist, drives the geopolitical reconfiguration of the country, opening new routes and opportunities for the drainage of products and regional integration.

With the continuation and success of these projects, Brazil can position itself as a strategic logistics hub, strengthening its position on the global stage and promoting sustainable and integrated development of its regions.
