The installation of the first rails of the VLT Carioca in 2015 was a milestone in urban engineering, highlighting the innovative technical solution of the APS system to preserve historical heritage and promote modal integration.

Every great city reveals its intelligence by the way it moves people. In Rio de Janeiro, the installation of the first rails of the Light Rail Vehicle (VLT) Carioca on March 27, 2015, marked a significant transformation in urban mobility. Workers from the VLT Carioca concessionaire laid 400 meters of rails on General Luiz Mendes de Morais Street, in the city center, beginning a new era of modern and efficient public transportation.

Each rail bar is 18 meters long, allowing adjustment in curves. This flexibility is essential for adapting the system to the existing road network. The VLT integrates seamlessly with other modes, such as buses, subway, and trains, facilitating the movement of citizens and tourists through the city.

The average speed of 17 km/h and the capacity of 420 passengers per car make the VLT Carioca an efficient transportation option, especially in densely populated areas. The APS (Alimentation by the Ground) system is a crucial innovation, eliminating the need for overhead catenaries and keeping the urban aesthetic clean and modern. Underground power boxes supply energy to the vehicles, ensuring that the historical landscape of Rio is preserved.

On-board energy banks allow vehicles to travel up to 400 meters without external power, offering a margin of safety and energy efficiency. With a width of 2.65 meters and a height of 2.17 meters, the VLT Carioca is designed to integrate harmoniously into the urban environment, providing comfort and efficiency to passengers.

The total cost of the VLT Carioca project, estimated at R$1.5 billion, reflects the significant investment in infrastructure and technology. The forecast is for 28 km of extension, 46 stations, and a fleet of 32 cars, creating a comprehensive and integrated transportation network. The voluntary ticket validation system, integrated with the Bilhete Único (Unified Ticket), simplifies access and use of the service.

The implementation of VLT Carioca is not just a matter of transportation, but also of urban requalification. The project is part of the Porto Maravilha Urban Operation, aiming to revitalize the central and port area of Rio. Integration with other means of transportation, such as Novo Rio Bus Station, Central do Brasil, Ferry Boats, Santos Dumont Airport, and BRTs, creates a cohesive and efficient network, improving citizens' quality of life.

The use of the APS system is particularly notable, as it allows the preservation of historical heritage. In Brasília, for example, the VLT is being implemented with the same system, respecting the heritage listing of the Pilot Plan, as pointed out by the portal <a href='https://www.metropoles.com/'>Metrópoles</a>. This approach demonstrates the versatility and importance of VLT in sensitive urban contexts, where architectural preservation is fundamental.

In São Paulo, the Bonde São Paulo project, which also uses the VLT system, is underway. The São Paulo City Hall concluded a comprehensive urban study, highlighting the positive impacts of VLT on the dynamics of the central region. The Bonde São Paulo, with two lines totaling approximately 12 km, will connect strategic neighborhoods, such as Bom Retiro and Brás, facilitating access to important cultural and economic points.

Regenerative braking, a technology used in VLTs, contributes to energy efficiency by recovering part of the energy dissipated during braking and storing it for future use. This reduces energy consumption and minimizes environmental impact, aligning with the sustainability goals of large cities.

The Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model adopted in VLT Carioca and other similar projects, such as in Brasília, is an effective strategy to attract private investments and ensure financial and operational sustainability. This model, which involves the Expression of Interest Procedure (PMI), allows the participation of specialized companies in the execution and maintenance of the system, ensuring high quality and efficiency.

The implementation of VLT Carioca and similar projects in other Brazilian cities, such as Brasília and São Paulo, highlights the importance of rail transport as a central technology for development, urban mobility, and productivity. These projects not only improve citizens' quality of life but also boost the local economy, increase property values, and promote social inclusion.

The integration of VLT with other transport systems, such as subway, buses, and bike lanes, is essential to create a more cohesive and functional mobility network. This approach, which prioritizes connectivity and accessibility, can be replicated in other regions, serving as a model for the modernization of urban transportation throughout the country.

The VLT Carioca, with its innovative technical solutions and modal integration, represents a significant advance in urban engineering. It not only improves mobility but also contributes to urban requalification and the preservation of historical heritage, becoming an example of how rail technology can transform our cities into more sustainable and livable environments.
