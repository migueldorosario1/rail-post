Without know-how, fiscal backing, or an environmental license, the pharaonic R$60 billion project insists on existing only on paper, revealing a vicious cycle of promises that national engineering cannot fulfill.

Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction.
The bullet train project between São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, valued at R$60 billion, is a striking case study of how Brazil insists on skipping stages without having mastered the previous ones. While the national rail network struggles at average speeds of 80 km/h for freight, the country flirts with the utopia of cutting 417 kilometers through mountains and asphalt at 320 km/h, a technology mastered by only four nations on the planet.
The gap between rhetoric and engineering reality is deep and painful. Despite technical advances and the hiring of consultancies since 2023, the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA/Rima) has not yet been submitted to Ibama, an essential document for obtaining the environmental license. Without this license, the promise of starting construction in 2028 remains uncertain.
As pointed out by a <a href="https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/brasil/promessa-do-pt-obras-adiadas-trem-bala-sao-paulo-rio-de-janeiro/" target="_blank">Gazeta do Povo report</a> that details the successive postponements, technological dependence is the crucial vulnerability of the project. Economist Cláudio Frischtak, president of Inter.B Consultoria, is categorical in stating that Brazilian railway engineering lacks any construction experience with high-speed trains (TAV), historically operating at a different level. Only China, France, Japan, and Spain possess real expertise in long-distance TAV, which condemns Brazil to a complete import of know-how, exponentially increasing the exchange rate and technical fragility of the enterprise.
The federal government, at least in its current discourse, seems to have partially woken up to the fiscal insanity of the undertaking. In 2025, the government officially launched the concessionaire selection notice (Notice No. 01/2025, published in the Official Gazette on March 12, 2025), with direct support from BNDES and a sovereign credit guarantee. However, the Ministry of Transport has already signaled that BNDES will act exclusively as a financial agent, without public capital contribution, a drastic shift from the direct subsidy promises made by Minister Renan Filho in 2023. The justification of TAV Brasil, supported by ticket sales between R$300 and R$500 to compete with the air shuttle, challenges any logic of economic viability without a robust and unlikely cross-subsidy.
Thomas Schoeffel, director-secretary of the Brazilian Railway Preservation Association, exposes another weakness by recalling that even Brazilian subways are essentially foreign products assembled in the country. A bullet train would require the technical crossing of the Serra do Mar and the resolution of complex urban interferences for which national engineering, lacking practical grounding, is simply not prepared. The illusion of an international consortium that magically transfers technology ignores that the learning curve is measured in decades, not in electoral years.
The technical, rational alternative, discarded due to political haste, highlights the delusion of the current project. A medium-speed train at 150 km/h, with shared track use for freight at night, would offer a superior mobility and logistics solution that is financially more palatable. Such a model, discussed during the Dilma Rousseff administration and summarily shelved, would allow two concessionaires, dual operating revenue, and gradual modernization of national engineering, without the quantum leap required by a TAV.
The planned route with four stations — São Paulo, São José dos Campos, Volta Redonda, and Rio de Janeiro — ignores the budgetary reality of a country in permanent fiscal crisis, as Frischtak well reminds when citing the Spanish case, built on billions in transfers from the European Union. The insistence on a pharaonic project, while the gauge and ballast of our freight railways remain stuck in the 19th century, is material proof of a vicious cycle that uses the rail as a soapbox and modernity as an electoral abstraction, leaving true developmental railway engineering abandoned on the platform.
