French innovations, such as low rail and ground-level power supply, are transforming the economics and urban impact of modern trams, with significant effects on projects in Brazil and Portugal, including the Bonde São Paulo and the Baixada Santista light rail.

Apart from the bicycle, the tram is the most ecological urban transport mode. France has been proving for three decades that precision engineering is what turns this vocation into operational reality. The country has 29 agglomerations equipped with modern tramway networks, a technical heritage that now crosses borders and influences light rail projects in Brazil and Portugal.

The 'rail bas', or low rail, requires only 42 centimeters of excavation depth, compared to 60 centimeters for the classic profile. This 18-centimeter saving avoids costly diversions of utility networks and drastically reduces the impact of works in densely occupied historic centers.

The multitubular technique embeds electrical and telecommunication cables directly into the track slab, eliminating deep side trenches and shortening the urban disruption zone. The running surface is modulated by context: floating concrete slab in the hypercenter, plain concrete in residential areas, and vegetated ballast on the outskirts to ensure soil permeability.

Traditional substations are being replaced by 'shelters' — prefabricated containers that arrive ready at the site and reduce the volume of concrete and installation time. Direct fastening of catenary poles to the track eliminates foundation blocks, freeing up the subsoil and cutting raw material consumption.

The compact maintenance center proposed by Eurovia/ETF occupies only 20,000 square meters, gathering stabling, washer, workshop and parking in a single rational polygon. Safety is ensured by a railway signaling system with a failure rate of less than 10⁻⁹ incidents per hour in normal mode, a reliability level typical of heavy metros.

The entire platform respects the UIC standard gauge of 1.435 meters, ensuring compatibility with manufacturers such as Alstom, Bombardier and CAF. Modern light rail vehicles, with full low floor and regenerative braking, return energy to the grid at every stop and reinforce the environmental vocation of the mode.

A study by Alstom and Carbone 4 reveals that a single tramway consist can carry the equivalent of 170 cars, occupying a tiny fraction of road space. The construction company Eurovia and its railway subsidiary ETF have 35 years of experience in this field, as detailed in the <a href='https://www.mundotrilhos.com/blog/20260711-tramway-frances-as-licoes-tecnicas-de-um-icone-da-requalificacao-urbana-de-baixo/' target='_blank'>technical dossier on tramways</a>.

The menu of French innovations is of direct interest to two Brazilian projects in gestation: the Bonde São Paulo, in the final phase of urban studies, and the Baixada Santista light rail, which will connect Barreiros to the Port of Santos. The application of low rail and compact substations would reduce the cost and implementation time in São Paulo's historic center, while the Santos route benefits from the reactivation of the exclusive right-of-way of the old Samaritá-Porto railway.

The Baixada Santista project, detailed by <a href='https://www.mundotrilhos.com/blog/20260709-vlt-da-baixada-santista-inovacoes-tecnicas-e-planejamento-integrado-para-a-mobil/' target='_blank'>SYSTRA Brazil's engineering</a>, foresees the adoption of APS technology — Alimentation Par le Sol — the same that debuted in Bordeaux and was replicated in the <a href='http://www.rio.rj.gov.br/web/guest/exibeconteudo?id=5267887' target='_blank'>VLT Carioca</a>, in Rio de Janeiro. With 1.8 million inhabitants and the largest port in South America, the region demands a transport system that combines capacity, reliability, and respect for the scenic heritage.

The APS powers the vehicle segment by segment, requiring batteries or supercapacitors for gaps without energized rail. This technology eliminates the overhead catenary, allowing the operation of wire-free consists on specific sections, a gesture of financial engineering on routes with multiple intersections and bridges. The Alstom light rail platform has already sold more than 1,500 vehicles with full low floor and a cruising speed of up to 104 km/h, proving that railway robustness is not incompatible with light insertion into the urban landscape.

The influence of the French model also echoes in Lisbon, where the new tram line 16E will connect Praça do Comércio to Parque Tejo, representing the first network expansion in over 70 years. With an investment of €160 million in rolling stock and an estimated construction cost of €298 million, line 16E takes advantage of the segregated right-of-way and traffic signal priority principles that characterize French tramways, according to projections by <a href='https://www.urban-transport-magazine.com/en/the-current-status-urban-rail-projects-for-lisbon/' target='_blank'>Urban Transport Magazine</a>.

The Linha Violeta, which will serve the municipalities of Odivelas and Loures, is a light metro/metro project, with 11.5 kilometers and 17 stations, three underground, two in cut-and-cover, and twelve at surface. The total investment is €527 million, of which €390 million come from the European Union, an indicator that EU funding recognizes light rail as a mature decarbonization technology.

The discussion between LRT and BRT loses strength when examining the total life-cycle cost of the infrastructure, which favors rail in high-density corridors and decades of intense service. Regenerative braking, reduced maintenance of the permanent way, and universal accessibility through full low floor turn light rail into a vector of energy efficiency and social equity.

The secret of success lies in the early integration between designers, builders and public authorities, in the design-build model that anticipates interfaces and eliminates improvisations. The system's modularity allows implementation in progressive phases, starting with a high-density section such as Barreiros-Porto, with the specification of fixed systems already sized for future expansion.

The French experience demonstrates that the tram is not a mere passenger carrier, but a powerful tool of urban 'aménagement' that reorders sidewalks, lighting, landscaping and furniture from facade to facade. It is this precision engineering, associated with a clear urban vision, that can return to Brazil and Portugal the tram culture as a lever for low-carbon development.
