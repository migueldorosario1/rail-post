The abandonment of rails is the cruelest metaphor of a country that insists on strangling its own productivity.

<p>Speed is also a form of civilization, and Brazil seems to have forgotten this maxim. The country that once believed in the Light Rail Vehicle (VLT) as an urban legacy ended up collecting abandoned construction sites, while the cargo that sustains GDP remains hostage to the road modal.</p><p>The most brutal example of this mismatch is in Mato Grosso. The 280 cars of the Cuiabá Light Rail, divided into 40 train sets, have been parked since 2013 in the same yard where they were unloaded, under private surveillance and without ever having transported a single passenger.</p>

<p>Only 30% of the physical works were executed, at a cost that has already exceeded R$ 1.066 billion to the state coffers. The substitution of BRT for Light Rail, later confessed as a maneuver to divert resources, resulted in the most expensive unfinished work of the 2014 World Cup, as <a href='https://www.bbc.com/portuguese/brasil-44320552' target='_blank'>documented by BBC Brasil</a> in a thorough investigation.</p><p>Former governor Silval Barbosa even covered the construction sites with grass to disguise the failure on the eve of the mega-event. Later, he admitted to the Federal Police that the political group received about R$ 18 million in kickbacks linked to the contract with the Consórcio VLT.</p>

<p>The wound of Cuiabá, however, does not erase the truth that engineering and economics insist on reaffirming: rails are infrastructure for development. In Brazil, only one in five cargoes goes by railway — somewhere between 18% and 21% of the transport matrix, according to estimates by Infra S.A. cited in <a href='https://oglobo.globo.com/economia/negocios/noticia/2026/07/29/no-brasil-so-uma-em-cada-cinco-cargas-segue-por-ferrovia-conheca-os-projetos-para-mudar-esse-cenario.ghtml' target='_blank'>a report by the newspaper O Globo</a>.</p><p>China, with its 162 thousand kilometers of rails, transports 3.9 billion tons of cargo annually. The United States, with a network of 225 thousand kilometers, moves another 2 billion tons, while Brazil languishes with 12 thousand operational kilometers.</p>

<p>The Brazilian logistics cost could fall up to 40% with a robust railway matrix, according to the National Confederation of Industry. However, the country lost half of its railway network over the last decades — from 30 thousand kilometers to the current 12 thousand — due to abandonment and lack of consistent public policy.</p><p>Now, the federal government tries to reverse the scenario with the National Railway Plan, which foresees investments close to R$ 100 billion and the concession of five large projects to the private sector. The East-West Corridor, which unites Fiol and Fico, will have more than 1,700 kilometers connecting the Center-West to Bahia's ports.</p>

<p>The Ferrogrão, with 933 kilometers between Sinop and Itaituba, promises to cheapen the outflow of grains through the Northern Arc. And the Southeastern Railway Ring, a 575-kilometer project linking the Port of Açu to Espírito Santo, inaugurates a model in which the Union finances part of the investments to guarantee viability, as detailed by <a href='https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/' target='_blank'>CNN Brasil</a> in coverage of the plan's launch.</p><p>The renegotiation of contracts with giants like Vale, Rumo and MRS Logística injected resources that make these undertakings viable. The mining company was willing to pay up to R$ 17 billion for the extension of the concessions of the Carajás Railway and the Vitória-Minas Railway.</p>

<p>In the sphere of continental integration, Brazil and China signed a memorandum of understanding to study a transcontinental railway that would connect the port of Ilhéus, in Bahia, to the Peruvian port of Chancay, on the Pacific. The route would cross Bahia, Goiás, Mato Grosso, Rondônia and Acre before reaching the Peruvian coast, as reported by <a href='https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/08/ferrovia-para-ligar-o-atlantico-ao-pacifico-veja-o-que-se-sabe-ate-agora-do-projeto-do-brasil-com-a-china.ghtml' target='_blank'>G1</a>.</p><p>Projections by the Peruvian government indicate that the new route would reduce the transport time of Brazilian cargo to Asia from 40 to 28 days. It is a structural change that repositions Brazil in the logistics geopolitics of the 21st century.</p>

<p>The National Railway Plan also includes the extension of the North-South Railway to the port of Vila do Conde, in Pará, creating an alternative to the saturated Carajás Railway. The Transnordestina, a work promised since 2010, finally has a delivery forecast and connection with the national network in Estreito, Maranhão.</p><p>BNDES has already approved R$ 55 billion for logistics since 2023 and can finance up to 80% of projects with terms of up to 40 years. The concession model has been improved, with sharing of geological risks and cost variations, correcting distortions that drove away international operators.</p>

<p>In the specific case of Light Rails, the lesson of Cuiabá needs to be absorbed with technical humility. Civil engineer Miguel Miranda, PhD in transport, warned that the modal was oversized for a metropolitan region of only one million inhabitants.</p><p>The projected capacity of 160 thousand passengers per day contrasted with the reality of a demand that would fit perfectly into a well-planned BRT. Political stubbornness cost R$ 1.066 billion and no passenger transported.</p>

<p>However, the stupidity of the Mato Grosso case does not condemn the technology itself. Cities like Santos and São Paulo are studying Light Rail corridors that combine intermediate capacity with lower deployment cost than underground subways and greater energy efficiency than diesel buses.</p><p>Electric traction on rails eliminates local emissions, reduces urban noise and organizes public space with a precision that asphalt will never achieve. Each kilowatt-hour applied to a Light Rail moves more useful mass with less friction and less wear.</p>

<p>Brazil needs to stop treating railways and subways as electoral merchandise and start treating them as infrastructure for development. The Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, has in his hands a pipeline of projects that can redefine the country's competitiveness, but the curse of the short term continues to haunt the offices.</p><p>Speed is also a form of civilization, and the hand of the Brazilian logistics clock has been stopped for more than two decades. To get back on track is, above all, an act of strategic intelligence.</p>
