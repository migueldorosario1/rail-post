While the country invests billions in transcontinental railways and national plans, urban rail mobility remains neglected.

Brazil is living a railway paradox: while signing billion-dollar agreements with China to link oceans and launching national plans of R$ 100 billion in freight railways, the LRT (Light Rail Transit) and urban rail transport systems remain on the sidelines of the national strategic debate. Speed is also a form of civilization, but the country's priority is still commodity flow, not low-carbon urban mobility.

In Brasília, the federal government advances railway infrastructure projects, but the emphasis is clearly on freight transport. The Brazil-China agreement for the transcontinental railway, which would depart from Ilhéus (BA) and arrive at the port of Chancay (Peru), crossing BA, GO, MT, RO, and AC, is an example. As reported by the agency portal <a href="https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2025/07/08/ferrovia-para-ligar-o-atlantico-ao-pacifico-veja-o-que-se-sabe-ate-agora-do-projeto-do-brasil-com-a-china.ghtml" target="_blank">G1</a>, the memorandum of understanding signed between Infra S.A. and the China Railway Economic Research and Planning Institute, with an initial duration of five years, aims to reduce the Brazil-Asia transport time from 40 to 28 days, facilitating the flow of Brazilian products, especially to Asia, where China is Brazil's main trading partner.

Meanwhile, the National Railway Plan foresees 4,700 km of new railways and R$ 100 billion in investments, as highlighted by <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>. The East-West corridor, composed of the West-East Integration Railway (Fiol) and the Center-West Integration Railway (Fico), will be approximately 2,400 km long. Other projects include the extension of the North-South Railway, with an additional 477 km to the port of Vila do Conde (PA), and the Southeast Railway Ring, about 300 km connecting Vitória (ES) to Itaboraí (RJ). Ferrogrão, with 933 km between Sinop (MT) and Itaituba (PA), faces pending issues in the Federal Supreme Court due to its impact on the Jamanxim National Park.

The Federal Government will assume 20% to 30% of the investments in the National Railway Plan projects, seeking to ensure their economic viability. However, the absence of any mention of urban LRT expansions in Brazil's major 2025 railway plans highlights the gap between freight infrastructure and urban rail mobility.

The SP-RJ high-speed train, with 417 km, a top speed of 320 km/h, and an estimated cost of R$ 60 billion, had its construction postponed to 2028, according to <a href="https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/brasil/promessa-do-pt-obras-adiadas-trem-bala-sao-paulo-rio-de-janeiro/" target="_blank">Gazeta do Povo</a>. TAV Brasil projects passenger operations starting in 2032, with tickets between R$ 300 and R$ 500. However, the project's EIA/Rima has not yet been submitted to Ibama, suggesting the timeline may change.

Eight railway auctions are scheduled for 2026, but industry insiders expect at most two to reach the B3 stock exchange. The Minas Rio Corridor would be the first auction of 2026, but the bidding notice has not yet been published, as reported by <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/infra/com-foco-em-ferrovias-governo-avanca-nos-primeiros-projetos/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>. The focus on shortlines, public calls for proposals, rebidding, and early concession renewals reflects the government's strategy to unlock investments and reorganize the existing network, but urban mobility continues to be relegated to the background.

Intermodality, essential for logistical efficiency, requires an integrated approach that considers both freight transport and urban mobility. Freight railways are crucial for economic development, but the lack of investment in LRTs and other urban rail systems indicates a limited vision. To balance its investments, Brazil needs to promote both commodity flow infrastructure and low-carbon urban mobility, meeting the needs of cities and contributing to environmental sustainability. Modern railway technology has the potential to transform urban mobility, offering efficient, sustainable, and low-impact solutions. LRTs, with their capacity to transport large numbers of passengers, reduce congestion and CO2 emissions, should be integrated into national strategic plans. After all, urban mobility is as fundamental to the country's development as freight infrastructure.
