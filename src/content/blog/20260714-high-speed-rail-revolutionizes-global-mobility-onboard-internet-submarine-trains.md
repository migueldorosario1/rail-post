The evolution of high-speed rail technology is transforming the way people travel, promoting greater efficiency, sustainability, and connectivity in various regions of the world.

Every major city reveals its intelligence by how it moves people. In this context, advances in the implementation of high-speed trains have shown a significant impact on urban and regional mobility, as well as contributing to environmental sustainability. Countries like Germany, China, the European Union (EU), and Canada are at the forefront of these innovations, with ambitious projects that promise to revolutionize the transportation sector.

In Germany, Deutsche Bahn has been investing in technologies that enhance the passenger experience. An example is the Gigabit Innovation Track (GINT) project, funded by the German federal government with 6.4 million euros. GINT aims to test internet connections up to five gigabits per second on trains. This project not only improves the passenger experience but also prepares the infrastructure for the future Rail Mobile Communication System (FRMCS), which is crucial for the digitalization and interoperability of the railway system. According to Deutsche Bahn, the initiative makes the train a space where passengers can work and be entertained without interruptions, increasing the attractiveness of rail transport.

China, on the other hand, is advancing in a megaproject that will redefine subaqueous engineering. The plan involves building a 123-kilometer underwater railway tunnel in the Bohai Strait, connecting the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas. This project, estimated at over R$ 224 billion, will reduce travel time between Dalian and Yantai from more than six hours to just 40 minutes. Besides benefiting passengers, the submarine train promises to boost cargo transportation and economic integration between strategic regions. Although challenging due to seismic activity and underwater pressure, the project reflects the Chinese government's priority in investing in cutting-edge infrastructure to strengthen the economy and national connectivity.

The European Union (EU) also has ambitious plans for an interconnected and interoperable high-speed rail network. The recently launched High-Speed Rail Action Plan sets investment strategies, infrastructure priorities, and regulatory evolutions to make the train a faster and greener alternative to short-distance flights. The goals include reducing travel times between major cities, such as Berlin–Copenhagen to four hours and Paris–Rome to less than nine hours. The plan also aims for lower fares and simplified reservations, encouraging competition among operators and facilitating the purchase of cross-border tickets. The EU expects to invest around 345 billion euros to complete the project, with expected benefits to the public valued at about 750 billion euros.

In Canada, the TGV project between Quebec and Toronto is gaining momentum. With speeds of up to 300 km/h, the train will reduce travel time between the two cities from five to three hours, providing a significant gain of two hours. The investment, estimated at up to 80 billion Canadian dollars, includes the construction of a dedicated line, eliminating mixed traffic issues with freight trains. International companies, including SNCF, Systra, Keolis, RATP Dev, Meridiam, Renfe Operadora, Deutsche Bahn, and Alstom, are involved in this project, highlighting the global importance of high-speed technology.

These projects illustrate the growing importance of high-speed trains in promoting mobility and sustainability. Interoperability, energy efficiency, and modernization of telecommunications are crucial elements for the success of these initiatives. Although Brazil is still in an early stage in this area, observing and adopting these technologies could be fundamental for developing a modern and efficient railway infrastructure in the country, aligned with global best practices.
