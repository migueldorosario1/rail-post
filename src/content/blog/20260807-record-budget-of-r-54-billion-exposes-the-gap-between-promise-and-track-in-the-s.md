Largest allocation in history contrasts with the Line 17-Gold, a branch that should have been ready for the 2014 World Cup and still awaits inauguration.

<p>Every great city reveals its intelligence by the way it moves people. São Paulo, with its 12 million inhabitants squeezed between hills and floodplains, decided to bet big on rail in 2026.</p>
<p>The budget of São Paulo's Metro reached the unprecedented mark of R$ 5.4 billion, a 12% jump over the R$ 4.8 billion executed in 2025. The figure, sanctioned by Governor Tarcísio de Freitas of the Republicans party, represents the largest allocation ever destined for the metro system in a single fiscal year.</p>
<p>Of this amount, R$ 2.59 billion goes to the expansion of Line 2-Green, which will extend 13.8 kilometers of new tracks to the municipality of Guarulhos. The first stage, up to Penha, has already surpassed 55% completion and foresees eight new stations before advancing with five more stops in the second phase.</p>
<p>The connection to Guarulhos carries powerful symbolism: it will be the first time that São Paulo's Metro breaks the capital's border to serve the state's second largest municipality. The region, which already has the Line 13-Jade train since 2018, will finally have direct metro integration with the central network.</p>
<p>In the East Zone, Line 15-Silver, a monorail that cuts through the city's most populous vector, will receive R$ 1.03 billion in 2026. The stretch between Ipiranga and Hospital Cidade Tiradentes concentrates efforts to increase the capacity of a mode that already transports hundreds of thousands of passengers daily.</p>
<p>The monorail, a technology that dispenses deep tunnels and drastically reduces expropriation costs, operates on elevated concrete beams with rubber-tired trains. Line 15-Silver is the first in Brazil to adopt the system on a large scale, with stations such as Vila Prudente and São Mateus already consolidated.</p>
<p>The great wound of the system, however, goes by the name of Line 17-Gold. With R$ 836.3 million reserved for 2026, the 6.7-kilometer branch with eight stations should finally connect Congonhas Airport to Morumbi Station on Line 9-Emerald.</p>
<p>The inauguration is scheduled for March 2026, as detailed by the <a href='https://exame.com/brasil/expansao-das-linhas-2-15-e-entrega-da-17-plano-de-r-54-bi-do-metro-de-sp/' target='_blank'>survey by Exame magazine</a> on the company's budget. The delivery was originally expected for the 2014 World Cup, which means more than a decade of accumulated delay.</p>
<p>The branch will be operated by Motiva Trilhos, but the construction was the responsibility of the Metro itself, in an arrangement that mixes public investment with private operation. Line 17-Gold uses monorail technology similar to Line 15-Silver, but on a much shorter route and with high complexity of urban insertion.</p>
<p>The persistence of works dragged out for decades is nothing new in Brazilian mobility engineering. The country masters the construction of metros and urban trains, but stumbles on fiscal regularity, environmental licensing, and the alternation of priorities between state administrations.</p>
<p>The case of Line 17-Gold is instructive: a medium-capacity mode that should have relieved access to the busiest airport in the southern hemisphere back in 2014. Twelve years later, the passenger who disembarks at Congonhas continues to rely on buses, taxis, or ride-hailing apps to reach the metro network.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, the Metro advances on modernization fronts that go far beyond physical expansion. The installation of the CBTC system, acronym for Communication-Based Train Control, is underway, which replaces fixed signaling with continuous radio communication between trains and the control center.</p>
<p>CBTC allows reducing the interval between trains, increasing the frequency and capacity of lines without the need for new permanent way works. The technology is also a prerequisite for operation with platform screen doors, another item in the modernization package that should be accelerated in 2026.</p>
<p>Platform screen doors, glass barriers that isolate the track from the station environment, increase passenger safety and improve energy efficiency. They are already a reality in systems such as Dubai and Tokyo, and São Paulo's Metro is beginning to adopt them systematically.</p>
<p>The purchase of 63 new trains is also underway, including 19 monorail units for Line 15-Silver and 44 conventional trains for lines 1-Blue, 2-Green, and 3-Red. Of these 19 monorails, 15 have already been delivered, reinforcing the fleet serving the East Zone.</p>
<p>The conventional trains operating on the system's oldest lines date, in part, from the 1970s and 1980s. Fleet renewal is essential to maintain operational reliability and reduce energy consumption, as the new models incorporate more efficient induction motors.</p>
<p>All this effort is part of an even broader state package, which mobilizes R$ 57 billion for 50 kilometers of metro expansion. Added to this are R$ 14 billion for 22 kilometers of railway network, as revealed by the <a href='https://timesbrasil.com.br/brasil/sp-anuncia-mais-de-r-50-bi-para-expansao-do-metro/' target='_blank'>coverage by Times Brasil</a> of the São Paulo state government's announcement.</p>
<p>The map of interventions includes lines 4-Yellow, 6-Orange, 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire, and 13-Jade, in addition to the already mentioned 2-Green, 15-Silver, and 17-Gold. In total, 72 additional kilometers of rail transport are planned, a figure that changes the scale of the metropolitan system.</p>
<p>Line 6-Orange, run by the concessionaire Linha Uni through a Public-Private Partnership, is another crucial chapter of this expansion. With 15.3 kilometers and 15 stations, it will connect Brasilândia to the city center, with partial operation scheduled for October 2026 and full operation in 2027.</p>
<p>The extension of Line 4-Yellow outside the capital also deserves mention, as it breaks a historical barrier of the system. The line that currently runs from Luz to Vila Sônia will cross municipal boundaries, connecting the metro network to neighboring cities in an unprecedented move.</p>
<p>The record budget of 2026 is, therefore, a two-faced portrait of Brazilian urban mobility engineering. On one hand, the technical capacity to design, contract, and execute simultaneous works on multiple fronts with modern technologies such as CBTC and monorail.</p>
<p>On the other, the long shadow of unfulfilled promises, symbolized by Line 17-Gold. The permanent way that will connect Congonhas to Morumbi is proof that new money can coexist with old delay, in a country where the rail often proves more difficult to lay than asphalt.</p>
<p>The passenger density that the São Paulo Metro transports daily—more than 4 million people on weekdays—makes each kilometer of expansion a vector of productivity and urban equity. Each new station on the outskirts shortens distances that buses and cars overcome with hardship and pollution.</p>
<p>Investment in rails is, above all, a declaration of principles about the city's future. São Paulo has decided that its growth will go through tunnels, elevated beams, and transfers, even if the price is living with the specter of works that take longer than they should.</p>
