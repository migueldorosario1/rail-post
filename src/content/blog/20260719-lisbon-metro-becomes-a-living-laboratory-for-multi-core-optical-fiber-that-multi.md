LUMIRing project installs 26 kilometers of next-generation optical fiber in Green Line tunnels and puts Portugal at the forefront of global communications.

<p>'We are creating a testbed that can scale enormously, transforming Lisbon into a geographical and technological reference point,' stated Jorge Costa, vice-rector of ISCTE - Lisbon University Institute, as he revealed the outlines of the Lisbon Underground Multicore Fiber Ring. Officially inaugurated in November, LUMIRing has turned the Lisbon Metro's underground infrastructure into the world's largest multi-core optical fiber testbed.</p>

<p>The backbone of the experiment entirely occupies the Green Line, in the 26-kilometer stretch connecting Odivelas to Rato, as <a href='https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/2025/11/03/metropolitano-de-lisboa-e-iscte-inauguram-maior-banco-de-testes-de-fibra-otica-multinucleo-do-mundo-3/' target='_blank'>detailed in the official Lisbon Metro statement</a>. In total, more than 26 kilometers of multi-core fiber were installed, ensuring ideal conditions for long-range comparative tests.</p>

<p>The heart of the innovation lies in the internal architecture of the cables, which abandon the traditional single core and now feature four or seven independent cores within the same fiber. This physical multiplication of channels increases data transmission capacity up to seven times compared to current technology, dramatically expanding traffic volume without requiring new excavations or additional ducts.</p>

<p>The total project investment amounts to 1.4 million euros, with 588 thousand euros coming from the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF), channeled through the Lisbon 2030 program. The financial engineering reveals a model of cooperation between academia, public authorities, and industry that can be replicated in other cities with consolidated metro networks.</p>

<p>The execution brought together heavy industrial partners such as Germany's Heraeus Covantics, Italy's Tratos Cavi, and Japan's NICT (National Institute of Information and Communication Technology), a public ICT research agency. Practical tests and demonstrations with multi-core fiber systems in a realistic underground environment are already scheduled to take place throughout 2026.</p>

<p>The acting president of the Lisbon Metro, Maria Helena Campos, stressed that the collaboration represents 'a unique opportunity to test next-generation technologies in a real urban environment with operational scale.' The leader reinforced that this step is decisive to validate new-generation multi-core optical fibers, solutions still little tested on a global scale.</p>

<p>Vice-rector Jorge Costa added that 'this terrestrial testbed will transform Portugal into a global optical telecommunications hub,' attracting investments in artificial intelligence and digital transformation. Major global operators such as AT&T, Deutsche Telekom, and Vodafone have already expressed investment interest, which should generate highly qualified jobs based in Lisbon.</p>

<p>Adolfo Cartaxo, the project's scientific leader, explained that the collaboration with Japan's NICT is a concrete example of the interest aroused by LUMIRing. The partnership includes large-scale demonstrations of multi-core fiber systems within the tunnels, taking advantage of the unique conditions of stable temperature, absence of solar interference, and physical protection that the underground offers.</p>

<p>For the Lisbon Metro, the practical impact goes far beyond pure research and can accelerate the digitalization of operations. The exponential increase in transmission capacity will allow the integration of control systems, real-time communication, onboard security, and intelligent fleet management with a robustness impossible in conventional fiber networks.</p>

<p>The Lisbon model carries a powerful message for cities like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, whose metro networks also extend for dozens of underground kilometers. The already excavated tunnel infrastructure can serve as a testing platform for critical connectivity technologies, generating revenue, technical knowledge, and international prominence for Brazilian metro companies.</p>

<p>By choosing the Green Line as a living laboratory, Lisbon demonstrated that urban mobility and the digital revolution can literally run on the same track. LUMIRing is not a futuristic ornament: it is an engineering asset that transforms the city's underground into a strategic layer of competitive innovation for the coming decades.</p>

<p>The Portuguese experience shows that a metro does not only transport passengers, but can also carry the future of global communications in its concrete belly. Every train crossing the Green Line today runs alongside one of the world's most advanced test infrastructures, silent and invisible, yet capable of redefining the limits of data traffic.</p>
