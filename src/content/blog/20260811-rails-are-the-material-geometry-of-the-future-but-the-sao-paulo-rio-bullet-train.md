Record investment in the São Paulo metro contrasts with the eternal postponement of the high-speed rail link between the country's two largest metropolises.

Rails are the material geometry of the future. In cities that lead global productivity, rail transport defines the efficiency of mobility and the very organization of urban space.

São Paulo, with its R$ 57 billion package for metro expansion, tries to align itself with this logic. The state government announced the extension of 50 km of metro and 22 km of railway, involving strategic lines such as Line 2-Green, Line 6-Orange, and Line 15-Silver, as reported by <a href='https://timesbrasil.com.br/brasil/sp-anuncia-mais-de-r-50-bi-para-expansao-do-metro/' target='_blank'>Times Brasil | CNBC</a>.

Line 2-Green, known as the "Paulista Line," is under study to reach Guarulhos after decades of waiting. Line 15-Silver, a monorail in the eastern zone, advances to Ipiranga and Cidade Tiradentes, while Line 17-Gold will finally connect Congonhas Airport to the metro network.

The figure includes US$ 425 million in loans authorized by Governor Tarcísio de Freitas for lines 2-Green and 6-Orange. The plan also foresees the unprecedented extension of Line 4-Yellow beyond the capital, breaking a historic barrier of the system, although it is still in the study phase.

In 2026 alone, the budget for the São Paulo Metro will reach R$ 5.4 billion, the highest in history. According to a survey by <a href='https://exame.com/brasil/expansao-das-linhas-2-15-e-entrega-da-17-plano-de-r-54-bi-do-metro-de-sp/' target='_blank'>Exame</a>, R$ 2.59 billion will go to the expansion of Line 2-Green, R$ 1.03 billion to Line 15-Silver, and R$ 836.3 million to the completion of Line 17-Gold.

The amounts are part of a R$ 33 billion program for metro works in the state, including the purchase of 63 new trains and the installation of CBTC signaling systems. The modernization includes platform screen doors, which increase safety and operational fluidity.

This volume of investment demonstrates that rails, when treated as development infrastructure, reorganize metropolitan competitiveness. Each new kilometer of line reduces the commuting time of the workforce and expands access to economic opportunities.

But the contrast with the bullet train project between São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro is brutal. While the São Paulo metro reaches record budgets, the 417 km high-speed railway between the country's two largest economies has been postponed once again.

The works, which had been announced for 2027, now have a start date only in 2028. The estimated cost, according to the latest updates, exceeds R$ 100 billion, and the federal government no longer foresees direct participation in financing, according to <a href='https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/brasil/promessa-do-pt-obras-adiadas-trem-bala-sao-paulo-rio-de-janeiro/' target='_blank'>Gazeta do Povo</a>.

TAV Brasil, the company authorized by ANTT to build and operate the rail axis for up to 99 years, projects tickets between R$ 300 and R$ 500. Commercial operation is promised for 2032, but the project still lacks an environmental license and the EIA/Rima has not even been submitted to Ibama.

Economist Cláudio Frischtak, president of Inter.B Consultoria Internacional de Negócios, is skeptical. "It is an extremely expensive project and very difficult to justify from an economic point of view," he stated, recalling that Brazil has never built high-speed trains.

Frischtak remembers that the more rational alternative would have been a medium-speed train, around 150 km/h, with shared use of the line for freight at night. Then-President Dilma Rousseff dismissed the proposal, which could have made the corridor viable without relying on massive subsidies.

The executive secretary of the Brazilian Railway Preservation Association, Thomaz Schoeffel, reinforces the national technical fragility. "Brazil does not have railway engineering prepared for this. Not even our metros are, in fact, Brazilian — at most, they are assembled in the country," he stated.

The Law of Railways, sanctioned in 2021, paved the way for private authorizations, but the bullet train remains an urban legend. Since 2007, when the Lula government launched the project in the PAC with a promise of operation for the 2014 World Cup, successive auctions have failed due to lack of interested parties.

While one type of rail slips, another advances. The National Railway Plan, with R$ 100 billion in investments and 4.7 thousand km of new railways, was presented to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by the Minister of Transport, Renan Filho, and should be launched soon, according to <a href='https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/' target='_blank'>CNN Brasil</a>.

The plan foresees the concession of five major undertakings, such as the East-West Corridor, the extension of the North-South Railway, the Southeast Railway Ring, the Transnordestina, and the Ferrogrão. The Union will fund 20% to 30% of the contributions, using resources from the renegotiation of railway concessions.

The Ferrogrão, with 933 km between Sinop and Itaituba, is the most challenging project, stalled in the Supreme Federal Court due to environmental impacts. Nevertheless, it signals a change in mindset: rails are beginning to be treated as state policy, not as an electoral promise.

Urban and productive modernity demands that rails leave the drawing board with the same speed as the trains they intend to operate. The contrast between the expanding São Paulo metro and the eternally postponed bullet train shows that Brazil has not yet aligned the geometry of the future with the execution of the present.

Rails are, indeed, the material geometry of the future. But without construction, licensing, and financing, they become just an abstraction on the horizon — and the country misses the train.
