The progress of a nation is measured by the efficiency with which its rails move its production. With the start of works on the Arauco railway branch, Brazil takes a decisive step towards modernization and decarbonization of cargo transport.

The progress of a nation is measured by the efficiency with which its rails move its production. This maxim becomes concrete with the beginning of works on the first railway project to leave the drawing board under the authorization model in Brazil, located in Três Lagoas, Mato Grosso do Sul. This railway branch, part of the Sucuriú Project by Arauco Suzano, represents a milestone for private investments in railway infrastructure and for the decarbonization of cargo transport in the country.

The railway branch in question has a 54 km extension, with 45 km of main tracks and 9 km of internal tracks. This strategic stretch will connect the world's largest single-stage pulp mill to the North Network, operated by Rumo, allowing flow to the Port of Santos (SP). The choice for this mode is not casual; the railway offers significant advantages in terms of efficiency and sustainability, especially for massive volumes such as pulp.

The total investment in the Sucuriú Project is US$ 4.6 billion, with R$ 2.4 billion specifically destined for railway infrastructure. This amount reflects Arauco Suzano's commitment to modernization and logistical optimization, aiming to increase the competitiveness and sustainability of its operations. The forecast is that the completion of this branch will occur in the second half of 2027, with works having been initiated in February 2025.

The adoption of railway transport for pulp promises to reduce CO₂ emissions by 94%, removing approximately 190 truck trips/day from the highways. This environmental impact is crucial, especially at a time when decarbonization of transport modes is a global priority. Additionally, cargo flow safety is substantially increased, reducing risks associated with road transport.

The connection with the North Network, operated by Rumo, is fundamental for the success of this project. The North Network, which uses broad gauge, allows efficient integration with the existing network, ensuring fluidity of transport from the mill to the main port of Latin America. The synergy between railway infrastructure and port logistics is essential for the sector's competitiveness, facilitating access to international markets.

The railway authorization model, launched in 2021, is an innovative instrument that allows private companies to propose, execute, and operate railway projects with the approval of the National Land Transport Agency (ANTT). This model seeks to accelerate the expansion of the railway network, encouraging private investments and promoting regional integration. Since the program's launch, several proposals have been submitted, but the VLI project in Mato Grosso (Cuiabá-Santarém Railway, initial phase) is the first private branch effectively under construction under authorization.

The construction of this railway branch is not just a technological advancement, but also a significant economic boost for the region. The work will generate jobs and stimulate the local productive chain, contributing to the socioeconomic development of Três Lagoas and adjacent areas. Furthermore, the presence of modern and efficient infrastructure can attract new investments, strengthening the regional and national economy.

The Arauco Suzano project is an example of how private initiative can collaborate with the government to promote modernization and sustainability of cargo transport in Brazil. The integration between the mill, the railway branch, and the Port of Santos creates an efficient logistical chain, capable of meeting the growing demand of international markets. This public-private cooperation model is fundamental for the advancement of railway infrastructure in the country, aligning with development and sustainability goals.

The implementation of this railway branch in Mato Grosso do Sul is a symbolic milestone for the future of cargo transport in Brazil. The choice for a more efficient and sustainable transport mode not only benefits the company, but also contributes to the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions, aligning with global climate mitigation goals. The continuity of this authorization model and the expansion of the railway network are crucial for the transformation of the country's transport matrix, promoting the competitiveness and sustainability of the Brazilian logistics sector.

The resumption of railway transport as a strategic alternative for long distances and large cargo volumes is an important step for Brazil's development. The combination of private investments, public planning, and market demand is fundamental for the expansion of railway infrastructure, strengthening integration between production regions and ports. This project inaugurates a new chapter in the history of railways in Brazil, marking the beginning of an era of innovation and sustainability in cargo transport.
