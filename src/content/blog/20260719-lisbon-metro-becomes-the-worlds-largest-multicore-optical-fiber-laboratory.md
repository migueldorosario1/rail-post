The rails are the material geometry of the future, and the Lisbon Metro is at the forefront of technological innovation. This project not only increases data transmission capacity but also contributes to building smarter and more sustainable cities.

The rails are the material geometry of the future. In Lisbon, Portugal, the underground infrastructure of the Lisbon Metro (ML) is being transformed into a living laboratory to test the most advanced multicore optical fiber technologies. The project, named LUMIRing (Lisbon Underground Multicore Fiber Ring), represents a significant leap in the field of next-generation optical communications.

The initiative uses 26 km of the underground infrastructure of the Yellow Line (Odivelas–Rato) of the Lisbon Metro. More than 1900 km of multicore optical fiber have been installed, of which 728 km are of the same multicore fiber type. These fibers, with 4 and 7 cores, promise to multiply the current transmission capacity, revolutionizing the way data is transmitted and processed.

The choice of the Lisbon Metro as the site for this project is not coincidental. The underground infrastructure offers unique conditions to test and validate new communication solutions in a real environment. This "living laboratory" allows experimenting with high-capacity optical fibers, which can be applied in control and communication systems, in addition to contributing to the digitalization and intelligent management of the metro network.

Multicore optical fiber is an innovative technology that promises to significantly increase data transmission capacity. With multiple cores, these fibers enable the transmission of much larger data volumes compared to conventional fibers. This is especially relevant in an urban context, where the demand for connectivity and transmission speed continues to grow exponentially.

The LUMIRing project is not limited to local benefits. With the planned tests with NICT (Japan) in 2026, the underground fiber ring in Lisbon will become a world-scale testbed. This international collaboration reinforces Portugal's position as a center of innovation in optical communications, attracting investments and talent from all over the world.

For Maria Helena Campos, Acting President of the Lisbon Metro, "this collaboration represents a unique opportunity for testing state-of-the-art technologies in a real urban environment with operational scale. It constitutes a decisive step for the Lisbon Metro in the development and validation of next-generation multicore optical fibers, using cutting-edge technological solutions still little tested on a global scale."

ISCTE Vice-Rector Jorge Costa emphasizes that "this land-based testbed will transform Portugal into a global center for optical telecommunications, attracting investments in communication technologies, Artificial Intelligence, and digital transformation." The shared vision of academic institutions and public transport operators demonstrates the strategic importance of this project for the country's technological and economic development.

Adolfo Cartaxo, the scientific leader of the project, adds that "the collaboration already agreed with the National Institute of Information and Communication Technology (NICT), a Japanese public agency for research in information and communication technologies, to carry out tests and demonstration of large-scale multicore fiber systems in a realistic underground environment throughout 2026, is a concrete example of this interest in the LUMIRing testbed."

The transformation of the Lisbon Metro into a living laboratory of technological innovation is not just a local achievement, but also an inspiration for other metro systems around the world. Although the project is exclusively Portuguese, its applications and results may influence future initiatives in cities like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, where the modernization and efficiency of transport infrastructure are crucial priorities.

The installation of multicore optical fibers in the Lisbon Metro is not limited to immediate technical improvements. In the long term, these innovations can contribute to the creation of smart and resilient cities, aligned with the principles of digital transformation and sustainable development. The integration of advanced technologies in public transport systems improves operational efficiency, safety, and user experience, positioning Lisbon as a model of urban innovation.

The LUMIRing initiative reinforces the role of the Lisbon Metro as a modern and innovative operator, focused on energy efficiency, sustainability, and decarbonization. By putting technology at the service of mobility and urban progress, the Lisbon Metro reaffirms its centrality in the mobility ecosystem of the Metropolitan Area, consolidating itself as a key player in building smart and resilient cities.

This pioneering project is not only a victory for science and technology, but also a catalyst for strategic development in the capital region. By positioning Lisbon at the forefront of experimentation in optical communications, LUMIRing strengthens the city's ability to attract talent, companies, and international investment, consolidating it as a hub of scientific and technological excellence.

The economic and social impact of LUMIRing is substantial. In addition to boosting economic competitiveness, the initiative contributes to a smarter, more sustainable, and interconnected Lisbon, where applied research translates into real benefits for citizens. The union of academia, public sector, and industry in a collaborative ecosystem lays the foundations for a more efficient and resilient urban future, transforming the Portuguese capital into a true model of global innovation and progress.
