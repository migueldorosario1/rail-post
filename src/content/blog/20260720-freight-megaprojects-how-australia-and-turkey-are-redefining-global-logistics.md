Ongoing railway megaprojects in Australia and Turkey are transforming freight logistics, offering valuable lessons for Brazil. With massive investments and advanced technologies, these projects demonstrate the strategic importance of railways in economic development and emission reduction.

The energy efficiency of trains offers predictability that highways cannot replicate. This is one of the main reasons why railway megaprojects are becoming pillars of global logistics. In Australia, the Inland Rail is an emblematic example of this movement. The 1,600 km logistics corridor between Melbourne and Brisbane aims to replace more than 100 trucks per train, significantly reducing emissions and relieving congested highways. The project, valued at around A$31 billion, requires complex engineering interventions, including the reconstruction of bridges and the adaptation of tunnels. According to the O Cafezinho agency, the Inland Rail represents a strategic bet to integrate agribusiness from the interior to Pacific ports, strengthening the country's export capacity.

The Inland Rail is not the only ambitious project redefining freight logistics. In Turkey, a large-scale project is underway, with an investment of 2 billion dollars. The project involves the construction of 127 km of line, half of the route in tunnels, increasing freight capacity from 3 to 50 million tons annually. High-capacity electric trains promise to cut emissions and attract investments in logistics and manufacturing. The World Bank estimates the creation of 414,000 direct and indirect jobs, including technical training programs open to women, reinforcing the initiative's inclusive nature. The electrification and construction of tunnels guarantee continuous 24-hour operation, protecting the infrastructure against extreme weather conditions.

In addition, the Rail-Flow digital platform, developed by the German company LIFE FIT, is transforming the way cargo is moved in Europe. With more than 9,500 bookable routes, 37 operators in 32 countries, the platform reduces transport costs by up to 20% and saves 115,000 liters of diesel. Dominik Fürste, co-CEO and co-founder of Rail-Flow, highlights the importance of intermodality: "If it's easy, companies will migrate to the railway mode, reducing congestion and emissions." The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into the platform allows detecting failures in overhead traction lines, reducing derailments and disruptions. According to the GOV.UK agency, innovative projects such as the IntelliPan Network are being funded by the British government, demonstrating a commitment to modernizing railway infrastructure.

These international projects offer valuable lessons for Brazil, where the logistics matrix is still excessively road-based. Initiatives like the Petrocity corridor, linking the Center-West to the port of São Mateus in Espírito Santo, illustrate efforts to modernize Brazilian infrastructure. Although these projects are in early stages, they represent an important step toward more efficient and sustainable logistics.

The Brazilian challenge remains in scale and continuity. While Australia and Turkey execute works with defined goals, Brazil needs to overcome institutional fragmentation and accelerate regulatory decisions. The construction of efficient logistics corridors, combined with digital control and progressive electrification, can generate economies of scale impossible in the road mode. The future of global freight is built on rails, and countries that invest in railway infrastructure are defining their position on the economic map of the coming decades. Sensors, dispatch algorithms, and electrified systems transform the railway mode into the foundation of a low-carbon economy, strengthening the logistical sovereignty of nations.

Australia and Turkey demonstrate that investment in railway infrastructure is an investment in autonomy. For Brazil, the message is clear: without a dense and modern railway network, there is no sustainable competitiveness. The State must plan and execute these works as a national priority to break away from dependent and inefficient patterns. Rails represent a strategic choice for development, and countries that build them today define their position on the economic map of the coming decades.
