Record investment of R$5.4 billion in 2026 expands the subway network and incorporates cutting-edge technology to give time back to the population

When technique organizes space, society gains time to live. In the São Paulo Metropolitan Region, the largest budget ever allocated to the Metro turns this maxim into engineering, tracks, and years of waiting that are beginning to be shortened.

As reported by the <a href='https://exame.com/brasil/expansao-das-linhas-2-15-e-entrega-da-17-plano-de-r-54-bi-do-metro-de-sp/' target='_blank'>Exame portal</a>, the São Paulo state government has set aside R$5.4 billion for the company in 2026, a 12% jump from the R$4.8 billion in 2025. The amount finances the simultaneous expansion of three lines, the purchase of new trains, and the modernization of traffic control.

Line 2-Green leads the investment with R$2.59 billion allocated to extend the track from Vila Prudente to Guarulhos. There will be 13.8 kilometers of new track and eight stations in the first phase, bringing the country's largest city closer to the main international airport with rapid mass transit.

The second stage to the center of Guarulhos will add five more stops and consolidate a structural corridor for the eastern zone. The work has already surpassed 55% completion and mobilizes tunneling machines, elevated stations, and extensive logistics of concrete, steel, and embedded systems.

In the eastern zone, Line 15-Silver takes up R$1.03 billion to extend the monorail connecting Ipiranga to Cidade Tiradentes. The investment represents a jump of more than 60% compared to the previous budget and addresses the capacity bottleneck in the most populous vector of the metropolis.

Meanwhile, Line 17-Gold receives R$836.3 million to complete the pending stretch between Congonhas Airport and Morumbi Station, with delivery scheduled for March 2026. The branch, dreamed of since the 2014 World Cup, will finally stitch together the southern zone with a medium-capacity rail connection.

Part of the resources will be applied to intelligent systems such as CBTC, the English acronym for communication-based train control. This technology replaces old track circuits and allows trains to circulate with much shorter intervals, braking and accelerating with centimeter precision and without direct human interference.

Platform screen doors, integrated with CBTC, eliminate the risk of falling onto the tracks and stabilize boarding flow. The Metro is also advancing with the order of 63 new trains: 19 for the Line 15 monorail and 44 units for lines 1-Blue, 2-Green, and 3-Red, of which 15 have already been delivered.

All this effort is part of a state package of R$33 billion in metro-rail works, whose execution extends over several years. In 2025, the Metro achieved 89% of the planned, with R$4.51 billion actually spent, surpassing the 2024 mark and signaling maturity in contract management.

The São Paulo movement echoes, on a state scale, the priority that the federal government is beginning to give to rail transport. As revealed by <a href='https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/' target='_blank'>CNN Brasil</a>, the National Railway Plan foresees R$100 billion in investments to deploy 4.7 thousand kilometers of new railways, connecting ports, agricultural regions, and industrial centers.

In the urban environment, every real invested in subway reduces the invisible cost of congestion, which erodes productivity, burns fossil fuel, and steals hours of family time. The electric rail, with silent traction and digital control, is the most efficient answer to densify cities without suffocating them.

The governor of São Paulo, Tarcísio de Freitas (Republicans), combines this robust public investment plan with the strategy of concession of lines to the private sector, generating controversies about the governance model. Still, the scale of allocated resources demonstrates that the State has not relinquished its role as planner and financier of structuring mobility.

By accelerating the expansion of lines 2, 15, and 17 and injecting intelligence into the tracks with CBTC, the São Paulo Metro not only shortens physical distances. It returns to the metropolis the promise that technique, when well applied, is capable of organizing the territory and giving back time — the most scarce and democratic resource that exists — to millions of people every day.
