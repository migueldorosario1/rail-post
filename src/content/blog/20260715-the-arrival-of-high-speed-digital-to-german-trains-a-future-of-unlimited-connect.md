Modernity is not abstract. It has ballast, gauge, energy, and direction. In Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Germany, the reality of this statement takes on concrete contours with the inauguration of the Test Track for High-Speed Internet in Trains. The Gigabit Innovation Track (GINT) project, supported by 6.4 million euros from the German government, represents a milestone in the integration of advanced technology into railway transport.

GINT aims to meet the growing demand for high-quality connectivity on trains, where up to five gigabits per second are needed to ensure an uninterrupted signal experience. Deutsche Bahn, Ericsson, O2 Telefónica, and Vantage Towers are working together to test and implement this infrastructure, demonstrating the potential synergy between the railway industry and telecommunications.

To make this possible, Vantage Towers installed 13 masts in just one month, using an innovative assembly technique. These 15-meter-high masts are anchored to the ground with steel bars, eliminating the need for concrete foundations. Besides reducing environmental impact, this solution also accelerates the installation process, allowing for more efficient and sustainable deployment of the infrastructure.

The expectation is that the infrastructure will enable network sharing between the railway and telecommunications industries, facilitating the implementation of the Future Rail Mobile Communication System (FRMCS). This system, which combines various communication functions, is crucial for the evolution of digitalization in the railway sector, providing greater efficiency and safety in operations.

Collaboration among GINT partners, including the Federal Ministry of Digital Infrastructure and Transport (BMDV), local authorities, and the infrastructure operator Regio Infra Nordost (RIN), was crucial to the project's success. Preparation, development, planning, and construction were completed in less than eight months, a record time for such an initiative. According to Dr. Volker Wissing, Federal Minister for Digital and Transport, "from funding approval to operation in just eight months – this is the pace we need for network expansion."

Starting in spring 2024, the 'Advanced TrainLab', a converted former ICE train from Deutsche Bahn, will be used to test and optimize the facilities on the Test Track. The results of these tests, expected to be released by the end of 2024, should provide valuable insights for expanding the technology across the country. As highlighted by Dr. Daniela Gerd tom Markotten, DB Board Member for Digitalization and Technology, "we can bring high-speed mobile communications to the train and track in record time. This not only makes the train a rolling office or home cinema but also lays the foundation for future digitalization of rail traffic with FRMCS."

Valentina Daiber, Board Member for Legal and Corporate Affairs at O2 Telefónica, emphasized the importance of this project in redefining connectivity along railway lines. "The mobile infrastructure is moving closer to the tracks to reliably supply train travelers with mobile high-speed internet," she stated. Daniel Leimbach, CEO of Ericsson GmbH, highlighted the speed at which the technology was deployed, emphasizing the importance of pre-configuration and rapid delivery of equipment.

Christian Hillabrant, CEO of Vantage Towers, stressed the crucial role of shared infrastructure in accelerating network expansion. "By using the same infrastructure for both railway and mobile networks, we leverage valuable synergies in network expansion and conserve valuable resources," he said. The joint use of masts and antennas for both railway and telecommunication communication is an innovative solution that promises to boost the efficiency and sustainability of the sector.

The implementation of high-speed and advanced connectivity in trains is not just a matter of passenger comfort; it represents a significant step towards more sustainable and efficient mobility. With the ability to offer high-quality internet experiences, trains become an even more attractive option, encouraging the transition from less sustainable modes of transport, such as cars and airplanes, to rail transport.

Although Brazil is still in the early stages of adopting similar technologies, the German experience can serve as an inspiring model. Integrating high-speed and advanced connectivity solutions into trains could bring significant benefits to urban and regional mobility, as well as contribute to reducing carbon emissions and promoting more sustainable transport.

Railway modernity, with its ballast, gauge, energy, and direction, is increasingly aligned with contemporary needs for connectivity and sustainability. Projects like GINT demonstrate that, with innovation and collaboration, it is possible to transform the travel experience on trains, making it more efficient, comfortable, and ecologically responsible.
