Rails are the material geometry of the future, and the expansion of the metro in São Paulo is at the epicenter of this transformation.

The state government of São Paulo announced an investment of R$ 57 billion to expand the metro by 50 km and the rail network by 22 km, totaling 72 km of new rail-based transportation. This project includes Line 6-Orange, operated through a PPP with the concessionaire Uni Line (Acciona). Line 6-Orange will have 15.3 km and 15 stations, with partial operation expected to start in October 2026.

According to a BNDES study, Brazil's metro network needs to double from 376 km to 699 km by 2054, with an estimated investment of R$ 500 billion. The average cost of the metro can be up to R$ 1 billion per kilometer, while BRT costs around R$ 50 million per kilometer. These figures highlight the need for a strategic and integrated approach, where rails become the backbone of urban and productive mobility.

CRRC, the world's largest train manufacturer, is setting up a factory in Araraquara (SP) to produce trains for the São Paulo Metro and the Intercity Train starting from 2026. This initiative strengthens the national supply chain and promotes modal integration, connecting the metro with major national rail corridors such as Fiol, North-South, and Ferrogrão.

The expansion of the metro in São Paulo goes beyond a mere mobility project; it is a sovereign infrastructure for territorial, technological, and productive integration. The metro-rail network, consisting of underground lines, monorails, and trains, offers superior loading capacity and energy efficiency, essential for urban sustainability. Additionally, integration with major national rail corridors creates a synergy that enhances the country's productivity and competitiveness.

For example, Line 6-Orange will connect the North Zone (Brasilândia) to the Center (São Joaquim), addressing crucial mobility and urban development demands. The Public-Private Partnership (PPP) with Acciona demonstrates the economic and operational viability of this model, which combines public and private resources to deliver high-quality and efficient projects.

Investment in rails is not just about transportation; it is a national development strategy. CRRC, with its factory in Araraquara, will contribute to the formation of a robust industrial base, generating jobs and local technology. This aligns with the goal of strengthening the national rolling stock industry, creating global partnerships that enhance competitiveness and innovation.

The National Railway Plan, which envisages the concession of five large railway projects to the private sector, complements this vision. With nearly five thousand kilometers of new railways and an estimated investment of approximately R$ 100 billion, the plan aims to connect production areas to ports, improving logistics and product distribution. According to <a href='https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/' target='_blank'>CNN Brasil</a>, the federal government has already closed agreements with companies such as Rumo and MRS Logística, ensuring the economic viability of these ventures.

The expansion of the São Paulo metro and the construction of new railways in Brazil represent a significant step towards more resilient and efficient infrastructure. Rails, as the material geometry of the future, are shaping urban and productive development, providing sustainable and integrated mobility. With strategic investments and solid partnerships, Brazil is firmly moving towards a future on rails, where technology and innovation align with sovereignty and national prosperity.
