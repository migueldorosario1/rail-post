The government prepares to launch a colossal railway plan focused on commodities, revealing an abyss of priorities that strangles the mobility of millions of people in metropolises.

Speed is also a form of civilization. However, observing the dance of investments on rails in Brazil, it becomes evident that the civilization we refer to is, above all, that of soybeans and iron ore traveling the path to the ports.

While the Federal Government prepares the launch of a National Railway Plan estimated at R$100 billion, the São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro metros continue with elephantine expansions, hostages to complex public-private partnerships and the eternal dependence on financing that never lands on the signing pen of the crowded stations.

The new plan, detailed by Transport Minister Renan Filho to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, and reported by <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>, is the materialization of a logistical choice. There are 4,700 kilometers of new rails that will connect the grain-producing heartland in the Center-West to port terminals in the Northern Arc, through megaprojects such as Ferrogrão and the East-West Corridor.

Ferrogrão, with its 933 kilometers between Sinop (MT) and Itaituba (PA), is the ultimate symbol of this export ambition, even though it depends on a delicate conciliation in the Supreme Federal Court (STF) due to impacts on conservation units. To make this heavy engineering math viable, where each kilometer of railway costs on average R$27 million — more than three times the cost of a highway —, the State does not hesitate to subsidize 20% to 30% of the investment.

It is a perfectly rational choice from the agribusiness perspective: in 2025, 73% of all Brazilian rail freight, or 406.6 million tons, was iron ore. Railways are the backbone of commodity exports, a mode designed for monoculture and not for metropolitan mobility.

Meanwhile, the world's largest train manufacturer, Chinese state-owned CRRC, is setting up a factory in Jacareí (SP) to produce freight cars, not passenger cars, starting in 2025. The São Paulo-Rio bullet train (TAV), in turn, accumulates postponements and now promises a start of construction only for 2028, without an environmental license and with an estimated cost of R$60 billion that the current government already treats as a full burden on the private sector.

It is a project that never reconciles with the real Brazil, where the priority of rails is so distorted that the first project to get off the ground after five years of the railway authorization model (ANPP) is a 54-kilometer branch of the Chilean Arauco in Mato Grosso do Sul, focused on pulp transportation. According to <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/infra/apos-cinco-anos-primeira-ferrovia-por-autorizacao-comeca-a-sair-do-papel/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>, the Arauco project is a symbolic milestone that does not deceive: regulatory agility is reserved for freight transport, not for the locomotion of ordinary citizens.

The imbalance is brutal. While discussions continue about mixed gauge to integrate export corridors and a design maximum speed of over 300 km/h for a bullet train that does not exist, the inhabitants of megacities suffer in trains that move at 80 km/h, when they are not stopped between overcrowded stations.

The National Railway Plan, with its East-West Corridor and the extension of the North-South line to the port of Vila do Conde (PA), is a piece of financial engineering aimed at the profit of international trade, shaped to attract Chinese capital that desires low-cost food security. The CCCC, a Chinese state-owned company, has already signaled interest in projects such as Fico-Fiol, closing the cycle of dependence on grain exports.

What is seen in the budgets is the exact translation of a country that decided to be the breadbasket of the world, but that despises the right to the city of its own population, a paradox precisely documented by <a href="https://investnews.com.br/negocios/brasil-tenta-de-novo-expandir-as-ferrovias-mas-ainda-falta-combinar-com-os-chineses/amp/" target="_blank">InvestNews</a> in showing that expansion attempts systematically ignore passengers.

The metro stations that promise to relieve the hellish traffic of São Paulo and Rio advance at a bureaucratic EIA/Rima pace and fiscal insecurity, while the government celebrates the renegotiation of concessions with giants like Vale, which will pay up to R$17 billion to the Union, but will continue operating railways designed for extremely heavy loads. Civilizational speed, in Brazil, is an arrow shot at the heart of urban inequality, where the rail that carries the ton is worth more than the rail that carries the worker.
