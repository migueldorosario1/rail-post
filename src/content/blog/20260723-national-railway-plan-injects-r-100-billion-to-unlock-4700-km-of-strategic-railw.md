The country seeking to break free from truck dependency is building a new steel backbone, connecting agribusiness and mining to ports with the precision that only rails can provide.

When technique organizes space, society gains time to live. The federal government finally unlocked a historic gear by announcing the launch of the National Railway Plan, a program with a financial scale of approximately R$ 100 billion aimed at rewriting Brazil's logistical geography with the addition of 4,700 kilometers of new rail lines.

The initiative, presented to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by Transport Minister Renan Filho, represents a turning point in the struggle to lift Brazil from its chronic dependence on road transport. As reported by <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-investimento-de-r-100-bi-governo-lancara-plano-nacional-de-ferrovias/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a>, the federal government is willing to assume between 20% and 30% of the investments to ensure the economic viability of the concessions, a pragmatic recognition that the cost of building a railway, often prohibitive for the private sector operating alone, demands a state push to get off the drawing board.

The backbone of this plan rests on five structuring projects. The first, a titan of logistical engineering, is the East-West Corridor, which consists of the junction of the West-East Integration Railway (Fiol) with the Center-West Integration Railway (Fico) to form a continuous stretch of about 2,400 kilometers between Ilhéus, in Bahia, and Lucas do Rio Verde, in Mato Grosso. This steel artery was designed to drain grain and mineral production directly to the coast, reducing the "Brazil cost" that erodes the competitiveness of producers in the international market.

Fico, currently under construction by Vale as a counterpart for the extension of its concessions, will be extended to the heart of Mato Grosso's agribusiness, while Fiol awaits the conclusion of its third section. The ministry's strategy is to build a significant part of Fiol with public funds, leaving the future concessionaire responsible for completing the work and fully constructing Fiol 3, in an auction scheduled for August.

Another crucial vector of the plan is the extension of the North-South Railway. Currently, the railway ends in Açailândia, Maranhão, where it connects to Vale's Carajás Railway (EFC), a line saturated by ore and with little spare capacity for the record harvests of the Center-West. To break this bottleneck, 477 kilometers of new tracks will take trains to the port of Vila do Conde, in Pará, creating a high-capacity logistical alternative for the Northern Arc and relieving pressure on the ports of the South and Southeast.

The integration of the network in the Southeast also gains an unprecedented chapter with the Southeast Railway Ring. With a route of approximately 300 kilometers between Vitória (ES) and Itaboraí (RJ), the project will allow the operational connection of the Vitória-Minas Railway (EFVM) to the MRS Logística network, eliminating a historical discontinuity that forced cargo to switch modes or take inefficient routes. It is the stitching together of a network that was born fragmented by 20th-century mining interests, now being rationalized for the multi-market logistics of the 21st century.

Ferrogrão, with its projected 933 kilometers between Sinop (MT) and Itaituba (PA), emerges as the most challenging venture in the portfolio. The project depends on a conciliation at the Supreme Federal Court due to potential impacts on the Jamanxim National Park. The complexity of taking structuring projects out of the drawer after years of paralysis requires a careful and transparent approach, involving various actors and interests.

In addition to the heavy-cargo giants, the plan injects oxygen into projects that have suffered decades of delay. The Transnordestina, a work that drags on like a monument to inefficiency, now has a delivery forecast for 2031, with the government committing to granting its 600-kilometer connection with the North-South Railway to Estreito (MA). The renegotiation of contracts with giants such as Rumo, MRS, and Vale also paves the financial path, with the mining company willing to pay up to R$ 17 billion for the extension of the EFC and EFVM contracts, a backing that fuels the cash flow for new investments.

The ecosystem is not limited to mega-structures, opening space for the concept of shortlines, smaller networks that the government intends to tender through public calls, in a process similar to that of the port sector. ANTT's director-general, Guilherme Sampaio, explained to <a href="https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/infra/com-foco-em-ferrovias-governo-avanca-nos-primeiros-projetos/" target="_blank">CNN Brasil</a> that the idea is to map returned corridors and reactivate them with a focus on cargo, using a model that speeds up the analysis of projects that do not require public resources.

Beneath the surface of the mileage statistics lies a silent revolution: the transition from an export economy that drags itself along roads to one that glides on broad-gauge rails. The timeline for 2026, although ambitious with the forecast of eight auctions, is already beginning to show the marks of political realism with the delay of the Minas Rio Corridor, whose bidding notice was postponed, but the mere materialization of a consistent pipeline signals to the market that the country will no longer fool around with its main infrastructure deficit.
