While the bullet train accumulates delays, São Paulo digitizes tunnels, extends rails to Guarulhos and orders 63 new trains in the largest metro cycle in history.

São Paulo Metro mobilizes a capital budget of R$5.4 billion in 2026. This amount is 12% higher than the R$4.8 billion of the previous year and represents the highest figure ever recorded by the company. The sum simultaneously supports the expansion of three lines, the purchase of 63 new trains and the digitization of control systems — an unparalleled combination in the history of the country's metro investments.

According to official data compiled by Exame, the record budget reflects the state government's decision to keep multiple construction fronts active. Speed is also a form of civilization, and it depends on tunnels advancing under São Paulo's soil without interruption, with drilling equipment operating 24 hours a day.

The flagship of the plan is the expansion of Line 2-Green. It will receive R$2.59 billion this year to extend its tracks by 13.8 kilometers between Vila Prudente and Guarulhos. This is the first time in the system's history that the heavy metro will cross the capital's border. The project includes eight new stations in the initial phase and five more in the following stage.

The engineering of this extension is not trivial. The route crosses densely urbanized areas of the east side, requires deep tunnels in sedimentary terrain and will demand the complete installation of CBTC (Communication-Based Train Control). This system replaces fixed signaling with continuous communication via digital radio, allowing minimal headways calculated in real time.

While Line 2 advances eastward, Line 15-Silver (monorail) consumes R$1.03 billion in 2026 to connect Ipiranga to Hospital Cidade Tiradentes. The monorail, often underestimated in public debate, operates with elevated concrete beams, distributed traction electric motors and reduced curvature radius. These characteristics make it suitable for narrow urban corridors where a conventional underground metro would be economically unfeasible.

The third relevant front is Line 17-Gold, with R$836.3 million allocated to the completion of the segment between Congonhas Airport and Morumbi Station. The branch line, whose inauguration is scheduled for March 2026, has 6.7 kilometers and eight stations. The project, originally promised for the 2014 World Cup, only now reaches technical delivery conditions, after successive revisions and replacement of the traction system.

The modernization of the lines in operation is also part of the package. This includes the installation of platform screen doors and the purchase of 63 new trains. Of this total, 19 units are destined for Line 15-Silver — of which 15 have already been delivered — and 44 will serve Lines 1-Blue, 2-Green and 3-Red. The fleet renewal reduces energy consumption per passenger-kilometer and increases operational reliability, directly affecting the lives of millions of São Paulo residents.

The onboard electronics of these new trains represent a generational leap. AC traction with IGBT inverters, real-time diagnostic systems and native integration with CBTC eliminate subsequent adaptations. Fleet renewal improves energy efficiency and reliability, crucial metrics for urban transport.

Governor Tarcísio de Freitas (Republicans) enacted a law in December 2025 authorizing loans of up to US$425 million, equivalent to approximately R$2.2 billion, from multilateral lenders and private banks for Lines 2-Green and 6-Orange. Line 6-Orange, a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) with the concessionaire Move São Paulo (consortium led by Acciona/Mota-Engil), totals 15.3 underground kilometers and 15 stations. Partial operation is scheduled for October 2026.

The R$5.4 billion budget for 2026 is not just a number inflated by contractual adjustments. It represents real capex in concreting, system supply and rolling stock, with a proven execution of 89% of the planned amount in 2025 (R$4.51 billion out of R$4.8 billion). The previous record of R$4.26 billion, in 2024, had already been surpassed the following year, indicating a consistent acceleration curve.

Intermodal integration is the silent guiding thread of these works. Line 2-Green will connect to Line 15-Silver at Vila Prudente, Line 17-Gold will have transfers with Line 9-Emerald at Morumbi and with Line 5-Lilac at Campo Belo, and Line 6-Orange will stitch the northern zone to the expanded center. Each integration node reduces transfers, decreases travel time and redistributes the passenger load on the network.

Contrary to what nostalgic discourses about trams and steam trains suggest, the rail of the 21st century is made of ready-mix concrete, heat-treated steel, fiber optics and control chips. What São Paulo is doing in 2026 is applying the state of the art of railway engineering to an urban fabric of 22 million inhabitants, with financing tied to physical schedules and delivery targets.

São Paulo's bet on the metro is not just a response to congestion, but a development policy that articulates national industry, technical training and long-term planning. While the country debates railways as a distant promise, the construction sites of Line 2, Line 15 and Line 17 show that Brazil already knows how to build high-capacity mass transit — when there is political decision and financial engineering to sustain it.
