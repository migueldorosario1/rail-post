The R$33 billion works cycle includes 63 new trains, CBTC technology, platform screen doors and the first metro line to cross the capital's borders towards Guarulhos

Every great city reveals its intelligence by the way it moves people. In this sense, 2026 exposes a São Paulo that decides to swap the inertia of collapse for the concrete of heavy engineering, with the most ambitious cycle ever seen in the São Paulo Metro.

The state Executive has sanctioned a budget of R$5.4 billion for the company this year, a 12% increase over the R$4.8 billion in 2025 and the largest annual amount ever allocated to the system. It is not an isolated promise, but the most robust slice of an active works package valued at R$33 billion, with multi-year execution that redefines the geography of the largest metropolis in South America.

The most eloquent demonstration of this financial and technical muscle is on Line 2-Green, which advances 13.8 kilometers of new tracks between Vila Prudente and Guarulhos. According to <a href='https://exame.com/brasil/expansao-das-linhas-2-15-e-entrega-da-17-plano-de-r-54-bi-do-metro-de-sp/' target='_blank'>a report published by Exame</a>, the first phase of the extension, which stretches to Penha, has already surpassed 55% completion and will receive R$2.59 billion in 2026 alone, compared to R$2.06 billion in the previous year.

The work embodies a symbolism of rupture that goes beyond the tracks and reinforced concrete. For the first time in history, a São Paulo metro line will cross the city limits, connecting directly to the state's second most populous municipality and redrawing the urban expansion vector of the East Zone.

The second largest volume of resources, R$1.03 billion, goes to Line 15-Silver, the monorail that articulates the densely populated fabric of the East Zone between Ipiranga and Hospital Cidade Tiradentes. The 2026 investment is far above the R$629.5 million foreseen in the 2025 budget and aims to expand the capacity of a branch whose technology demands millimeter tolerances in permanent way and rolling stock.

At the other end of the city, the chronicle of Line 17-Gold is moving toward the conclusion of a wait that has dragged on since the 2014 World Cup. With R$836.3 million in this year's budget, the stretch between Congonhas Airport and Morumbi Station is scheduled to open in March 2026 and will be operated by Motiva Trilhos after the Metro delivers the infrastructure.

The technological dimension of this investment cycle, however, is not measured only in additional kilometers of line. The modernization tender includes the implementation of the CBTC (Communication-Based Train Control) system, which replaces the old fixed signaling with continuous high-precision communication between the control center and the trains, allowing reduction of headway to minimum intervals and increasing train frequency without excavating a single extra tunnel.

Simultaneously, the installation of platform screen doors (PSD) advances, physical barriers that isolate the permanent way and eliminate the risk of passengers falling onto the tracks, an indispensable condition for very high frequency operations. These two systems engineering fronts go hand in hand and are what separate a metro of the past from a metro operating at maximum efficiency.

The purchase of 63 new trains proves the materiality of this transformation. Of this total, 19 units are destined for Line 15-Silver — with 15 already delivered — and 44 trains will renew and expand the fleet of Lines 1-Blue, 2-Green and 3-Red, the historic heart of the network that now undergoes deep modernization.

The financing of this leap in quality and extension combines different financial engineering architectures. Governor Tarcísio de Freitas sanctioned the law that authorizes the State to borrow up to US$425 million — approximately R$2.2 billion — for the works on Lines 2-Green and 6-Orange, according to <a href='https://timesbrasil.com.br/brasil/sp-anuncia-mais-de-r-50-bi-para-expansao-do-metro/' target='_blank'>information from Times Brasil</a>.

Line 6-Orange, in particular, represents the Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model achieving maturity in São Paulo's subsoil. With 15.3 kilometers in length and 15 stations connecting Brasilândia to the center, the forecast is for partial operation to begin in October 2026 under the responsibility of the concessionaire Linha Uni (Acciona), adding to the expansion effort as a private vector that complements direct public investment.

The overall plan figures size up the project's scale beyond the conventional metro system. The government mobilizes R$33 billion to expand approximately 50 kilometers of metro and a further R$14 billion for 22 kilometers of CPTM rail network, in a simultaneous task force of works on Lines 2-Green, 4-Yellow, 6-Orange, 15-Silver, 17-Gold, 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire and 13-Jade.

The Line 4-Yellow project, by the way, will bring another structural innovation by transposing the capital's limits, an unprecedented experience in the history of the São Paulo metro system. The decision to break the municipal barrier with two simultaneous fronts — Line 2-Green and Line 4-Yellow — signals that transportation planning has finally begun to see the real urban footprint, and not just the administrative border of the host city.

The design of the new fronts also reveals the gestation of an additional network layer with Lines 19-Celeste, 20-Pink and 22-Brown. These are projects that aim at the structural connection of Guarulhos, Osasco, ABC and Cotia, stretching the concept of intermodal integration far beyond current maps and demanding excavation capacity, deep stations and interfaces with bus terminals that have yet to be sized.

From the development policy perspective, what is observed is the transition of a metro company that for decades struggled to maintain the existing to an operator that plans and executes multiple fronts simultaneously. The record capital expenditure (CAPEX) budget of R$5.4 billion in 2026 is almost double what the company executed a few years ago, indicating that the project management and resource absorption capacity has matured.

The urban transformation resulting from this cycle is of the first order. Each new kilometer of metro in the East Zone modifies accessibility to the central city's income and reduces pressure on the bus fleet, while Line 6-Orange breaks the geographical barrier between the North Zone and the employment center with an entirely underground route.

Brazilian engineering may not master high-speed trains, but it is demonstrating full mastery over metro expansion in a dense urban environment contaminated by buried interferences. The challenge now is the hardest: sustain the multi-year execution pace and ensure that the increase in rail supply comes before the next mobility collapse of the metropolis.

The São Paulo Metro, after all, is proving in 2026 that well-calibrated public investment transforms the social geography of a city with the same intensity as it excavates a rock tunnel.
