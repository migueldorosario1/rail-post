The BNDES plan of R$500 billion is a snapshot of the delay, while São Paulo's concrete response with R$57 billion reveals the real cost of moving a metropolis into the future.

Every great city reveals its intelligence by the way it moves people. São Paulo, with its human mass of 22 million souls in the metropolitan region, has just launched one of the most ambitious engineering responses to mobility in the country.

The starting point is a national-scale diagnosis. A study by the Brazilian Development Bank (BNDES) mapped 194 public transport projects and concluded that Brazil will need to double its current subway network, from 376 kilometers to close to 700 kilometers, over the next three decades.

As the BNDES survey pointed out, medium-capacity systems, such as BRTs and light rail vehicles, will need to jump from 631 kilometers to an impressive 2,500 kilometers. The total bill to get these lines off the drawing board is estimated at R$500 billion by 2054, a figure that demonstrates the magnitude of the historical omission in high-capacity transport.

The financial equation is relentless. While a kilometer of BRT costs around R$50 million, the underground subway reaches figures of up to R$1 billion per kilometer, as explained by the BNDES Infrastructure Superintendent, Felipe Borim.

This monumental need for investment arises at a time of acute demand crisis. The number of public transport passengers shrank by 43% between 2014 and 2023, a hemorrhage of users who migrated to ride-hailing cars and motorcycles.

It is in this context of logistical blackout that the state of São Paulo announced a works front of R$57 billion to expand its subway network by approximately 50 kilometers. The package also includes R$14 billion for 22 kilometers of new urban train routes, totaling 72 additional kilometers of rail transport.

According to the São Paulo government's schedule release, the plan provides for simultaneous works on Metro lines 2-Green, 4-Yellow, 6-Orange, 15-Silver, and 17-Gold, as well as interventions on CPTM lines 11-Coral, 12-Sapphire, and 13-Jade. The operational complexity of tackling multiple fronts at the same time reveals a construction site of civilizational dimension.

The most symbolic feat of this expansion will be the unprecedented extension of Line 4-Yellow outside the São Paulo capital. For the first time in history, a subway train will cross the municipal limits of São Paulo, reaching Guarulhos, the second largest city in the state.

The embedded technology of these new sections illustrates the project's maturity. Line 6-Orange, a Public-Private Partnership with the concessionaire Linha Uni, will have 15.3 kilometers of tunnels with third-rail power supply, connecting Brasilândia to downtown. Its start of operation is scheduled for 2029, according to the ANTT tender notice and Linha Uni reports from January 2026.

Line 17-Gold adopts the monorail as a solution to overcome the consolidated urban fabric. However, it is important to note that Line 17-Gold was canceled in 2023 by the São Paulo government. There is no schedule, executive design, or current financing for it in 2026. Therefore, the mention of 'operation in 2026' is completely false.

The financing of this growth machine bears the signature of sophisticated credit engineering. Governor Tarcísio de Freitas enacted in December the law authorizing the state to raise up to US$425 million from private banks and multilateral organizations, an amount earmarked for the expansions of lines 2-Green and 6-Orange.

Interventions on axes such as Line 2-Green, which advances from Vila Prudente to Penha, and Line 15-Silver, the east zone monorail that will integrate with the future Ipiranga station, are technical responses to an urban fabric that has densified without the proper rail support. Each new underground kilometer challenges the geology of the São Paulo Basin, requires meticulous Environmental Impact Studies, and pacifies the relationship between the city and its subsoil.

The contrast between the BNDES national plan and the concreteness of São Paulo's tunnel boring machines is the guiding thread of a strategic reflection. While Brazil debates how to finance R$500 billion over three decades, São Paulo is already cracking the ground with steel and concrete, even at the cost of a billion per kilometer.

The intelligence of a metropolis is not measured only by the utopian horizon of its future network, but by the capacity to pierce the present with the tools of fiscal realism and technical courage. In the chess of mobility, the tracks advancing today write the most eloquent response to the silence of projects that remain on paper.
