With 1,304 km of tracks and an investment of R$ 29.4 billion, Nova Ferroeste is an innovative laboratory for heavy cargo transportation in the country, combining efficiency, sustainability, and new financing models.

Every major city reveals its intelligence by how it moves people. However, the same logic applies to the movement of goods, especially in a robust agricultural economy like Brazil's. The Nova Ferroeste project, which envisions a 1,304 km export corridor connecting Maracaju (MS) to the Port of Paranaguá (PR), with a branch line from Cascavel to Foz do Iguaçu, is a concrete example of this transformation. With a projected capacity of 38 million tons in the first year of full operation, the project reconfigures grain logistics in southern Brazil, reducing costs and environmental impacts.

The total investment of R$ 29.4 billion, with a 99-year concession via auction at B3, signals the strategic importance of the project. Each 100-car train replaces 357 trucks on the highways, according to coordinator Luiz Henrique Fagundes. This replacement not only alleviates road traffic but also reduces export costs by 28% on the Western axis, making rail transport a more viable and sustainable option. The project is eligible for Green Bond issuance, with verifiable environmental goals, attracting investors committed to sustainability.

Nova Ferroeste operates on a metric gauge, using diesel-electric locomotives and hopper cars for grains, configuring itself as a heavy-haul railway. The short line concept, applied to smaller branches that connect production hubs to main lines, allows for more efficient distribution of cargo. The revitalization of the yards in Cascavel and Guarapuava, with digital signaling, bridge reinforcement, and curve adjustments, prepares the infrastructure for longer and heavier trains, increasing transport capacity.

The global market for freight railway infrastructure is expected to grow from US$ 43.6 billion in 2026 to US$ 64.5 billion in 2036, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 4.0%. Brazil, with a projected growth rate of 4.8% per year, is well-positioned to take advantage of this trend. Projects like Nova Ferroeste are crucial for this development, as they not only modernize existing infrastructure but also create new export corridors, enhancing the country's competitiveness in the global market.

International experience, such as in India, shows that adopting new public-private partnership (PPP) models and hybrid annuity model (HAM) financing can accelerate the execution of large infrastructure projects. In India, the government divided the east-west corridor into approximately 10 smaller packages, attracting a wider range of investors and ensuring timely completion. Brazil, with the Nova Ferroeste auction at B3, follows a similar approach, combining initial public investment and private participation in operations, promising consistent results.

Multimodal integration, as seen in the São Francisco Waterway reactivation project, is another example of how different modes can be combined to create more efficient and sustainable logistics solutions. The waterway, spanning 1,371 km, connects Pirapora (MG) to Juazeiro (BA) and Petrolina (PE), allowing for the movement of up to 5 million tons in the first year of operation. The combination of waterway, railway, and roadways reduces costs, shortens delivery times, and decreases environmental impact, demonstrating the synergy between different means of transport.

State and federal governments are prioritizing investments in railway infrastructure, recognizing its crucial role in economic development and emission reduction. Nova Ferroeste, with its advanced technical features and financial innovations, is a Brazilian laboratory for how the country can finance, build, and operate rail transport infrastructure with scale, efficiency, and environmental responsibility. The project not only redefines the rail cargo financing model in Brazil but also sets a new standard for grain logistics in the south of the country. With a long-term concession and adherence to environmental goals, Nova Ferroeste points to a future where rails play a central role in sustainable development and economic competitiveness in Brazil.
