The second wave of Light Rail Vehicles (LRVs) in Brazil is in full swing, transforming urban mobility and injecting over R$ 10 billion in investments. With projects in several cities, the medium-capacity mode promises to revolutionize the way people travel.

Every major city reveals its intelligence by how it moves people. In Brazil, the second wave of Light Rail Vehicles (LRVs) is moving from a mere promise to reality, with signed contracts, published tenders, and ongoing works. Cities like Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Salvador, Santos, Fortaleza, Curitiba, Bauru, and Campina Grande are at the center of this transformation, which totals more than R$ 10 billion in investments.

The Gentileza Intermodal Terminal (TIG) in Rio de Janeiro is an emblematic example of this new generation. TIG integrates lines 1 and 4 of the Carioca LRV with the Transbrasil BRT and 14 bus lines, processing 130,000 passengers per day with a single fare. The total investment in the TIG corridor is R$ 2 billion, with R$ 532 million in federal funds. This project demonstrates the efficiency of intermodality, allowing passengers to make connections without physical or tariff barriers.

São Paulo advances with an innovative project. The São Paulo LRV plans 12 km on two lines in the historic center, using underground electrical power supply (APS). This system, which embeds a segmented third rail in segregated channels, preserves the listed landscape of the center, eliminating visible overhead catenary. The project, budgeted at R$ 1.4 billion from the Growth Acceleration Program (PAC), should be completed by 2027, depending on the speed of the tender modeling.

Salvador is also committed to modernizing its mobility. The Salvador LRV, with a budget of nearly R$ 4 billion, envisages three sections totaling 36.36 km, integrating with the metro at Águas Claras and Piatã stations. The project aims to fill the gap left by the deactivation of suburban trains in 2021, which pushed thousands of passengers onto overcrowded buses. According to <a href='https://mobilize.org.br/'>Mobilize Brasil</a>, the tender has already been published, and work is expected to begin soon.

In the Baixada Santista region, Phase 2 of the Santos LRV, with 8 km and 12 stations connecting the Barreiros-Porto branch to Valongo, was scheduled for delivery in July 2024. However, it is important to note that this date has passed and the deadline was not met. Phase 3, with 7.5 km and four stations heading towards the mainland area of São Vicente, is under construction at a cost of R$ 562 million. The project uses overhead catenary and dedicated channels, providing efficient and safe operation.

Fortaleza recently inaugurated a 2.4 km LRV branch, connecting the Airport to the Castelão neighborhood. Operating on diesel, the LRV integrates tourists and residents with the air terminal, improving accessibility and transport fluidity in the region. It is important to clarify that Castelão and Pinto Martins Airport are in adjacent neighborhoods, and the logic of the branch is focused on integration with the existing metro (South Line).

Curitiba, known for its BRT, embraces the LRV to rewrite the narrative of public transport. The feasibility study funded by BNDES envisages replacing the Boqueirão Axis BRT with a 10.6 km line with 27 stops and four terminals, extending to Afonso Pena International Airport. The estimated investment is R$ 2.5 billion, and the project represents a doctrinal shift, recognizing in the LRV a higher load capacity and energy efficiency compared to buses.

Infra S.A., a state-owned company linked to the Ministry of Transport, plays a crucial role in this transformation. The company was contracted by Bauru to conduct the Technical, Economic, and Environmental Feasibility Study (TEAF) for the LRV, which will have 28.8 km, three branches, and over 30 stations, utilizing the existing railway network. The city, with 380,000 inhabitants, hopes to alleviate pressure on the road system and boost urban requalification. Additionally, Infra S.A. is involved in the development of the LRV in Campina Grande, Paraíba, with the study expected to be delivered in the first half of 2026.

A peculiar case is the impasse of the 40 CAF wagons acquired in 2012 for the Cuiabá LRV, which remain stored outdoors in Várzea Grande (MT). While the Mato Grosso state government seeks solutions, the need to resolve this issue illustrates the importance of rigorous planning and coordination between federal entities.

The technical benefits of the LRV are undeniable. An electric LRV has a lifespan three times longer than a bus, zero local emission rate, and drastically lower maintenance costs. Platform doors, full fare integration, and low floors bring accessibility into the daily routine of the city, bringing predictability and comfort to users. The Brazilian Railway Industry Association (ABIFER) confirms that the Brazilian industry is prepared to meet these new projects with electric, hybrid, and green hydrogen traction solutions, positioning the country as a leader in sustainable mobility.

With the second wave of LRVs, Brazil rediscovers that medium-capacity transport is not a European luxury but an urban sovereignty requirement. Federal investments via PAC, PPP models, and the direct involvement of the state-owned Infra S.A. are transforming urban mobility, providing more efficient, sustainable, and accessible cities. The era of rails in Brazil is just beginning, and the future promises to be even more promising.
